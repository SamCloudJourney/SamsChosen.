# AI vs Vertical-Market Software — Moat Tripwires

*Equity research note. Date: 2026-05-31. Portfolio context: Constellation Software (TSE: CSU), Topicus (TSXV: TOI), ATOSS Software (ETR: AOF), Vitec Software (STO: VIT-B) — ~36% combined. CSU controls TOI (~21% of book is one CSU/TOI ecosystem; treat correlated, not diversified).*

**Central question:** Does generative AI erode the moats of vertical-market / serial-acquirer software businesses?

---

## 1. Bull case vs. bear case (stated fairly)

### Bull case — why vertical, regulation-embedded systems-of-record resist AI

- **The moat is a stack, and AI hits the layers unevenly.** A useful decomposition (LongYield, 5i) splits the VMS moat into ~5 layers: (1) switching costs / data-migration pain, (2) regulatory-compliance embeddedness, (3) accumulated organizational/workflow knowledge, (4) integration into mission-critical operations, and (5) feature completeness. AI compresses development cycles (3–5 years → 12–18 months), so it attacks **layer 5 (feature parity)** hardest — but layers 1–3 are "fundamentally immune": no model instantly migrates decades of institutional data or assumes regulatory continuity.
- **Mission-critical, low-ARPU, fragmented niches are unattractive to AI-native attackers.** These are tiny TAMs (a software product for, e.g., funeral homes, dental labs, municipal water utilities, Nordic property management). The economics that protected them from horizontal SaaS protect them from AI startups too: the prize is too small, the data too specialized, the buyer too conservative.
- **Customers are conservative and slow.** ATOSS and Vitec management both describe customers wanting benefits "without major disruptions" and adopting gradually. European / government / regulated buyers move slowly — Topicus's base skews exactly this way.
- **Proprietary, workflow-embedded data is the AI *advantage*, not the vulnerability.** The incumbent that owns the system of record can layer AI on top (forecasting, automation) and deepen lock-in. ATOSS already sells AI forecasting as a *deciding factor* in hospital deals.
- **The serial-acquirer model is anti-fragile to single-vertical disruption.** CSU/Topicus hold hundreds of independent businesses across hundreds of niches. Idiosyncratic disruption in any one is diversified away, and the capital-allocation engine can redeploy FCF regardless of which verticals win. Topicus is "not just a software company; it's a capital allocator."
- **Pricing power is being defended deliberately.** Vitec is explicitly re-architecting pricing toward *value-based* models so that customer efficiency gains from AI don't translate into seat/volume revenue loss — "fair compensation for the value provided, regardless of efficiency gains."

### Bear case — where AI genuinely threatens

- **Per-seat licensing is structurally exposed.** The broad 2026 software re-rating (the "SaaS reset" / "SaaSpocalypse") is built on agentic AI cannibalizing per-seat revenue. Several of these businesses still bill on users/seats. As headcount at customers flattens or falls, seat-based recurring revenue can erode even with zero logo churn.
- **System-of-record primacy is contested.** The a16z thesis: AI collapses the distance between intent and execution, turning systems of record into a "commodity persistence tier" beneath an agent layer. If the *interface* and *workflow logic* migrate to an AI layer, the incumbent's UI/feature moat thins even where its database survives.
- **Feature-parity barriers fall fast.** The very thing that let small VMS vendors out-execute generic competitors — deep, niche feature sets built over years — can now be replicated in 12–18 months by an AI-accelerated competitor. LongYield/5i estimate **25–35% of CSU's portfolio faces meaningful AI-native disruption risk within 5 years**.
- **Pricing/index headwinds are already visible at the most price-driven name.** Vitec's organic recurring growth is guided *down* (FY25 ~3–4% → Q1'26 guide ~2–3%) on weaker index-linked price escalators — not AI per se, but it removes the cushion that masks any future volume softness.
- **Maintenance/legacy lines are in managed decline.** Perpetual-license and on-prem maintenance lines are shrinking by design (cloud transition) — fine when cloud growth more than offsets, but it narrows the margin for error if cloud growth ever stalls.

**Synthesis:** Bull and bear are not mutually exclusive. The honest framing is *which layer, which vertical, which revenue model*. Regulated systems-of-record with non-seat pricing and sticky data are well-defended; seat-priced, feature-differentiated, low-regulation niches are the soft underbelly. The portfolio sits closer to the defended end — but is not immune, and the cushions are thinning.

## 2. Company-by-company (latest disclosed)

> Reporting note: organic growth and FX adjustments are disclosed differently by each. CSU/TOI report a blended organic figure plus a separate organic figure for **maintenance & other recurring** (the cleanest moat gauge). ATOSS is a single-product organic grower (no M&A engine) so reported growth ≈ organic. Vitec is a serial acquirer that breaks out organic.

### Constellation Software (CSU) — FY2025 (reported 2026-03-09)
- **Organic growth:** FY2025 **+4%** total organic (~+3% ex-FX); Q4'25 **+6%** organic (~+2% ex-FX). Total revenue $11,623m (+15% incl. M&A).
- **Recurring / maintenance:** Maintenance & other recurring grew **~+6% organic on a constant-currency basis** — the key tripwire line, and it held.
- **Net retention:** Not disclosed as a single NRR figure (CSU does not report NRR).
- **Earnings quality flag:** Net income to common −30% to $512m, driven by heavy deal activity / contingent-consideration and amortization, not core operating deterioration. M&A deployment remained very heavy.
- **AI commentary:** No formal President's-letter-style AI doctrine in this cycle. Leadership transition is material: **Mark Leonard resigned for health reasons (2025-09-25), Mark Miller (COO) became President.** Paraphrased Leonard view: the moat is *staying close to customers and delivering integrated value*, not efficiency. Proprietary, workflow-embedded data positioned as an AI advantage.
- *Sources:* GlobeNewswire/Yahoo FY25 release; TipRanks; LongYield; 5i Research.

### Topicus (TOI) — FY2025 (reported 2026-02-25)
- **Organic growth:** Q4'25 and FY2025 both **+4%** total organic. Revenue €1,552.3m FY (+20% incl. M&A); Q4 €436.8m (+20%).
- **Recurring / maintenance:** Recurring revenue **€1,097m = ~71% of total**, up +22% YoY; **maintenance & recurring organic growth +6%** in both Q4 and FY — squarely in the 5–7% band held over 3 years. **No sign of AI-driven deceleration.**
- **Net retention:** Not separately disclosed.
- **FCF:** FCFA2S +23% FY to €218.7m (+40% in Q4) — capital engine intact.
- **AI commentary:** Bulls point to three numbers as the live tripwires — (1) +6% recurring organic, (2) staff-expense ratio falling to 53.8% despite heavy M&A, (3) contingent considerations rising / impairments not. Management posture: European/government conservatism + high switching costs; "capital allocator" able to pivot.
- *Sources:* GlobeNewswire/Yahoo/Globe & Mail TOI Q4'25; Expanse Stocks; Potential Multibaggers.

### ATOSS Software (AOF) — FY2025
- **Organic growth:** Group revenue **+11%** to €189.3m (effectively all organic — ATOSS is a single-product grower, not an acquirer). 20th consecutive record year.
- **Recurring / ARR:** Recurring revenue **+18%** to €131.9m (**70% of total**). Cloud & subscriptions +28% to €92.7m (**49% of revenue**). Cloud & subs ARR **+28%** to €101.3m; total ARR **~€140m (+18%)**. EBIT margin **36%**.
- **Net retention:** **~111% FY2025** (target >110%); ~112% in Q1'26; ~116% / 2.9% cloud churn at YE2024.
- **Mix shift flag:** Perpetual licenses and on-prem **maintenance declining (~−3% maintenance in Q1'26)** and hardware −24% in Q1'26 — by design, as cloud cannibalizes. Cloud growth more than offsets.
- **AI commentary:** AI forecasting / planning / automation expanding scalability; **AI forecasting a "deciding factor" in hospital wins.** AI framed as offensive product capability. FY2026 guide ~€215m revenue, EBIT margin ≥32%.
- *Sources:* ATOSS IR FY2025; Yahoo Q4'25 & Q1'26 call highlights.

### Vitec Software (VIT-B) — FY2025 / Q1'26
- **Organic growth:** Q4'25 net sales SEK 983m (+6%); **Q1'26 organic +5%** total sales (revenue SEK 955m, +9% incl. M&A, −3% FX headwind).
- **Recurring revenue:** FY2025 recurring **+11%** to SEK 3,204m; Q4'25 recurring +8% to SEK 831m; subscription gross margin **90%+**. Q1'26 **subscription organic +6%**.
- **Net retention:** Not disclosed.
- **Churn:** **~1%** (low, stable).
- **Pricing flag (the one to watch):** Organic recurring growth **guided down to ~2–3% (Q1'26) from ~3–4%** prior year, attributed to **weaker index-linked price escalators (~1% lower pricing-index uplift)** — *not* attributed to AI/churn, but it thins the cushion.
- **AI commentary:** Management acknowledges the risk that AI makes customers more efficient → fewer seats/purchases, and is **moving to value-based pricing** to decouple revenue from efficiency gains. "AI allows us to move faster than our customers, who are cautious." AI impact currently shows up in product development/growth, not cost cuts.
- *Sources:* GuruFocus/Investing.com Vitec Q3'25, Q4'25, Q1'26 call highlights; Vitec IR; Simply Wall St.

**Cross-read:** All four show recurring/maintenance organic growth in a **+5% to +6%** band (CSU/TOI maintenance +6%, ATOSS recurring +18% headline but cloud-driven, Vitec subscription organic +6%) with churn where disclosed near ~1% (Vitec) and NRR >110% where disclosed (ATOSS). **As of the latest prints, there is no quantitative fingerprint of AI erosion in the core recurring lines.** The only softening is Vitec's *price-index*-driven recurring deceleration, which is macro/contractual, not demand/churn.

## 3. THE KEY DELIVERABLE — Tripwire framework

Measurable thresholds that distinguish **"moats holding"** from **"moats eroding."** Watch on the cadence each company reports. The logic: AI erosion shows up *first and most cleanly* in **organic maintenance/recurring growth and gross churn**, because that line strips out M&A, FX, and one-offs and directly measures whether existing customers keep paying. Total organic growth is a noisier, lagging confirmation.

| # | Metric | Where watched | Cadence | Holding (green) | Watch (amber) | Eroding — thesis flips (red) |
|---|--------|---------------|---------|-----------------|---------------|------------------------------|
| **T1** | **Organic maintenance / recurring growth (constant-FX)** — *primary signal* | CSU & TOI "maintenance & other recurring" organic; ATOSS recurring/ARR; Vitec subscription organic | Quarterly | ≥ +4% | +1% to +4% for 2 consecutive quarters | **≤ 0% for 2+ consecutive quarters, or a >3pp decel not explained by FX/price-index** |
| **T2** | **Gross logo / revenue churn** | Vitec (~1% disclosed); ATOSS cloud churn (~3%); CSU/TOI inferred from recurring organic | Quarterly / annual | Vitec ≤1.5%; ATOSS cloud ≤4% | Vitec 1.5–2.5%; ATOSS 4–6% | **Vitec >2.5%; ATOSS cloud churn >6%; any disclosed step-up framed as competitive loss** |
| **T3** | **Net retention (where disclosed)** | ATOSS NRR (~111%) | Quarterly | ≥ 108% | 103–108% | **< 103%, or two consecutive declining prints** |
| **T4** | **Total organic growth (ex-FX)** — *confirmation, lagging* | All four | Quarterly | ≥ +3% | +1% to +3% | **≤ 0% ex-FX sustained 2+ quarters with M&A unable to offset** |
| **T5** | **M&A deployment & pricing discipline** | CSU/TOI capital deployed + implied multiples paid; deal count | Quarterly / annual | Deployment steady/rising at stable multiples | Multiples paid creeping up while deal count falls | **Deployment falls sharply (no investable targets) OR multiples paid jump materially — signals either no opportunities or chasing growth to mask organic weakness** |
| **T6** | **Maintenance / legacy attrition vs. cloud offset** | ATOSS (maintenance −3%, hardware −24% Q1'26); CSU/TOI license vs. recurring | Quarterly | Cloud/subscription growth > legacy decline by wide margin | Offset margin narrowing | **Total recurring rolls over because cloud no longer offsets legacy decline** |
| **T7** | **Price-realization / index uplift** | Vitec pricing-index commentary; ATOSS/TOI list-price actions | Annual / on calls | Index + value uplift ≥ +2% | +1% to +2% | **Sub-+1% AND accompanied by volume/seat softness (price + volume both down = real demand erosion, not just CPI)** |
| **T8** | **Seat / volume vs. value mix** | Management commentary on seat-based revenue exposure; success of value-based pricing migration | Annual | Value-based pricing migration progressing; seat exposure shrinking | Seat revenue flat while customer headcount falls | **Seat-based recurring declines YoY because customers cut seats via AI productivity (the core bear mechanism)** |
| **T9** | **Win/loss & competitive narrative** | Management Q&A; "losses to AI-native competitor" mentions | Quarterly (qualitative) | No structural-loss mentions | Isolated mentions in 1–2 verticals | **Repeated, multi-vertical mentions of losing deals/renewals to AI-native entrants** |
| **T10** | **R&D / staff-cost ratio behavior** | TOI staff-expense ratio (53.8%); all four R&D% | Annual | Falling or stable while growth holds | Rising to defend feature parity | **R&D% must rise materially just to hold organic growth (feature-parity arms race) — margin-compressing erosion** |

**How to read it as a system:**
- **Single amber = noise.** Watch, don't act.
- **Thesis genuinely flips** when **T1 goes red** (organic recurring ≤0% for 2+ quarters, FX/price-index-adjusted) **AND** it is corroborated by at least one of **T2/T3 (churn up / NRR down)** or **T9 (competitive-loss narrative).** That combination means existing customers are leaving or shrinking *because of competition*, which is the only thing that actually breaks the model.
- **Capital-allocation tripwires (T5) are the serial-acquirer-specific kill switch:** for CSU/TOI the engine matters more than any single vertical. A *collapse in deployment* (no investable VMS targets because AI is killing the asset class) or *multiple inflation to chase growth* would be a thesis-level warning even if organic stays fine for a while.
- **Vitec is the early-warning canary on pricing (T7).** It is the most price-index-dependent and already guiding recurring growth down. If that deceleration migrates from "lower CPI uplift" to "lower volume," it's the first hard datapoint of the seat/efficiency bear mechanism (T8).
- **ATOSS is the canary on mix (T6) and NRR (T3)** — it discloses the cleanest NRR and is furthest through a cloud transition, so it shows the legacy-vs-cloud offset math most transparently.

## 4. Verdict and uncertainty

**Where the evidence currently points (2025–2026 prints): moats HOLDING, with one yellow flag.**

- Across all four, **core recurring/maintenance organic growth sits in a +5–6% band**, churn is ~1% where disclosed (Vitec), and NRR is >110% where disclosed (ATOSS). These are precisely the lines AI erosion would hit first, and **none of them show the fingerprint.** CSU/TOI maintenance organic at +6% constant-FX is the single most reassuring datapoint, because it is the metric an AI-driven churn wave would degrade earliest.
- The serial-acquirer engines are intact: TOI FCFA2S +23%, CSU still deploying heavily. Capital allocation — the real moat for CSU/TOI — shows no stress.
- AI so far reads as **offensive** for these names (ATOSS AI forecasting winning hospital deals; incumbents layering AI on proprietary data) more than as an attacker.

**The honest yellow flag:** **Vitec's recurring-growth deceleration (~3–4% → ~2–3%).** Management attributes it to weaker price-index escalators, *not* churn — and churn is indeed still ~1%. But it removes the cushion. If a real AI/seat-efficiency effect ever arrives, it will land on a business already running thinner organic growth, and it could be hard to distinguish from the price-index story in real time. **T1 + T7 + T8 on Vitec are the highest-priority watch.**

**Explicit uncertainties / what I cannot rule out:**
1. **Lag risk.** Switching costs mean erosion would appear with a *multi-year lag* — renewals are sticky, so today's healthy recurring lines could mask attrition that only surfaces at the next 3–5 year contract cycle. Current green prints are *consistent with* but do not *prove* durable moats.
2. **Disclosure gaps.** CSU, TOI, and Vitec do **not** publish a clean NRR; we infer retention from organic recurring growth. We are partly flying on a proxy.
3. **The 25–35% exposed slice (CSU/TOI) is real but unmeasured at the line-item level** — blended organic can stay positive while a quarter of the portfolio slowly bleeds, masked by the healthy 65–75%. The blended number is reassuring but not granular.
4. **Macro vs. AI confound.** Part of the SaaS re-rating is multiple compression / rate-driven, not fundamental AI erosion. Conversely, the price-index softness at Vitec is macro/CPI, not AI. Separating the two in the data requires the corroboration logic in Section 3 (don't trust a single line).
5. **2026 prints are early.** Agentic AI's effect on seat-based billing is, by most accounts, a 2026–2028 story still unfolding. Absence of evidence today is weak evidence of absence over a 5-year horizon.

**Bottom line for a concentrated holder:** The thesis is currently *supported* by the fundamentals — these are among the better-defended corners of software (regulated, niche, sticky data, conservative buyers, non-trivial non-seat revenue, and — for CSU/TOI — a capital engine that diversifies single-vertical risk). **Stay long, but instrument the position with the Section 3 tripwires.** The thesis does not flip on narrative or share-price drawdowns; it flips on **T1 going red and being corroborated by churn/NRR or a competitive-loss narrative.** Until then, treat 2026 as a monitoring year, with Vitec's pricing/recurring line and CSU/TOI's maintenance-organic line as the two gauges to watch every quarter.
