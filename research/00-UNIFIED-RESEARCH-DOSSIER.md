_Compiled 2026-05-31. Informational synthesis, not personalized financial advice._

## What this portfolio actually is

A deliberately constructed, concentrated **"quality compounder" book** built on four structural bets plus a defensive compliance sleeve. The businesses are genuinely high-quality with real moats and proven capital allocation — the issues are **thematic concentration and correlated valuation risk, not business quality**.

## Cluster-by-cluster

### Vertical-market software (36%) — the engine and the key debate
Constellation, Topicus, Vitec, ATOSS monetize mission-critical niche software with high switching costs and recycle cash into fragmented M&A (ATOSS being the organic exception). All three reports agree on the AI question: horizontal software is exposed, but **vertical, regulation-embedded systems of record** retain wide moats, and cheaper private-software valuations actually widen the acquisition pipeline. Tripwire: organic growth and churn, not headlines. **Structural note:** Constellation controls Topicus, so the ~21% in those two is effectively one capital-allocation ecosystem; correlated VMS exposure is really the full 36%.

### Life-sciences supply chain (24%) — picks-and-shovels, one shared cycle
Supplier/manufacturing positions rather than clinical risk. Bachem (peptide/oligo CDMO) and Sartorius (single-use bioprocessing, ~85% recurring consumables) share GLP-1/biologics exposure and one bioprocessing investment cycle — a GLP-1 air-pocket or overcapacity hits ~15% of the book together. Eckert & Ziegler (radiopharma isotopes, riding the radioligand-therapy wave toward a ~$26bn-by-2030 market) and Bioventix (diagnostic antibody royalties) are more idiosyncratic.

### Holding companies (18%) — discounted optionality with a trap risk
- **EXOR** (Agnelli) — ~50%+ discount to NAV near 52-week lows, buying back stock, rotating out of autos into healthcare (Philips, bioMérieux), ~€3.5–3.7bn dry powder from Iveco-related disposals; Lingotto (>$10bn AUM) returned ~40% in 2025.
- **Investor AB** (Wallenberg) — highest-quality, lowest-risk holdco; diversified blue-chips plus Patricia Industries, NAV ~SEK 355/share end-2025 (~SEK 367 by Q1 2026), tiny leverage.
- **Sofina** — most venture-exposed (≈45% private funds), recovering from prior write-downs, now net-cash, acting as a liquidity provider.
- **Hidden overlap:** all three hold European healthcare/industrial and private-growth assets; Investor AB's EQT stake plus Sofina duplicate private-market exposure.

### Defensive sleeve (12%)
- **Veralto** — water + product quality, Danaher-style, >60% recurring, ~23% margins, water-scarcity and packaging tailwinds; arguably the cleanest all-weather compounder in the book.
- **Secunet** — Germany's high-assurance cyber/defence partner; certification barriers lock out foreign rivals; Defence & Space now ~⅓ of revenue and growing fast; fortress balance sheet (negative Q1 2026 EBIT was front-loaded hiring/seasonality).

### Emerging Asia (10%)
- **India (ETF)** — liquid demographic/credit-growth bet; lagging, persistent foreign outflows, large-caps now below historical valuation.
- **Vietnam (VOF)** — frontier supply-chain-relocation bet; ~8% GDP growth, cheap (~10x forward), ~20%+ discount to NAV, but illiquid and higher-governance-risk.

## Strengths and gaps

**Strengths:** durable moats, recurring revenue, high returns on capital, disciplined capital allocators, owner alignment, and a de-rating that has handed historically cheap entry points on several names.

**Gap:** the book is stylistically monolithic — almost everything is a European-tilted quality compounder. Light on US large-cap breadth, mega-cap technology (ironically, given the AI risk it carries), energy/commodities, real assets, and direct financials. Currency is diversified but EUR-heavy.

_(Scenarios, not advice.)_

6. **Consider genuinely uncorrelated exposure** (energy/real assets, US/mega-cap, fixed income) if this is the whole portfolio.

# Portfolio — The Honest Read (Qualitative Assessment)

_Compiled 2026-05-31. Informational synthesis, not personalized financial advice._

## The core thesis

This is one of the more coherent and genuinely well-built portfolios around — and that coherence is **simultaneously its greatest strength and its single biggest vulnerability**. The distinction is the whole thing.

Most portfolios are accidents — a pile of names accumulated with no unifying idea. This is **a thesis expressed as a portfolio**. Almost every holding is the same kind of thing: a cash-generative business with high switching costs, run by an owner-operator or family whose incentives are aligned with patient, long-term value creation rather than next quarter. Constellation's acquisition flywheel, ATOSS's compliance-embedded software, Bachem's regulatory lock-in, EXOR's permanent capital, Veralto's razor-and-blade water franchise — these rhyme. That's a worldview, and a good one. Over a decade, owning durable compounders bought by disciplined allocators is about as sound a philosophy as exists in public markets.

## The catch: diversified-looking, not diversified

The same quality that makes it elegant makes it **fragile in one specific way: it isn't actually diversified, it just looks diversified**. Fifteen names across software, healthcare, holding companies, and emerging Asia feels like breadth, but underneath it's essentially **one bet** — that high-quality compounding works and the market eventually pays for it.

- When that style is in favor, everything rises together and you look like a genius.
- When it's out of favor (as recently), everything sits underwater together — which is what's happening now.
- **There is no genuine ballast:** no energy or real assets, no mega-cap tech, very little outside Europe, no cash or bonds to redeploy when things get cheap.

Built to win big in the world where quality compounding keeps working; **no hedge** for the world where that style stays unloved for five years.

## The AI question — the real fork in the road

Genuinely unresolved. The lean: the market is wrong to treat vertical, mission-critical, regulation-embedded software the way it treats generic horizontal software — the workflows Constellation, ATOSS and Topicus sell are precisely what AI **can't** cheaply rip out. But that's a judgment, not a fact, and the market's fear isn't stupid. The de-rating could be a gift, or the first innings of a real structural impairment that takes years to become obvious. **Nobody knows yet.**

Worth naming clearly:
- ~**36%** of the portfolio rides on the optimistic answer to an open question.
- ~**21%** sits in a single controlled ecosystem (Constellation runs Topicus).
- That's a high-conviction position — defensible **if deliberate and sized on purpose**. The honest thing to interrogate: is the concentration *chosen*, or did it *accrete* because you admire these businesses?

## De-rated is not the same as cheap

Cuts against the easy "everything's on sale, back up the truck" framing. Several names — Constellation and Sartorius both around **58x earnings** — have fallen a long way and are still **not cheap in absolute terms**. Part of what's happening is air coming out of genuinely stretched 2021 multiples, not a clean mispricing.

The clearest actual value is **narrower** than the broad weakness suggests:
- Holding-company discounts (**EXOR at a ~50% gap to NAV** is the standout).
- Arguably **ATOSS** after its collapse.
- With the highest-quality names like Veralto, you're paying up for quality and getting a defensive compounder — fine, but don't confuse it with bargain-hunting.

## The single most useful thought: the biggest risk is behavioral

The biggest risk to the outcome probably isn't any business in here — **it's behavioral**. This portfolio only delivers for someone who can:
- Hold through a multi-year stretch of looking wrong.
- Tune out the AI-doom narrative without becoming dogmatically dismissive of it.
- Keep **re-underwriting the businesses** rather than reacting to the prices.

The portfolio is already stress-testing exactly that temperament, with nearly everything below its highs. The real damage in a book like this almost never comes from the companies failing — it comes from **the holder capitulating at the bottom of a scare that later proves overblown**.

So the real question isn't "are these good businesses" (they mostly are) — it's: **"Do I have the stomach and the time horizon this strategy demands, and am I comfortable having no airbag if quality stays out of favor longer than I expect?"**

---

_Not a prediction or advice; not a financial advisor. The one thread most worth pulling: the AI-versus-vertical-software question, since ~36% of the outcome hinges on it — concretely distinguishing "the moats are holding" from "the moats are eroding" so you have real tripwires rather than vibes._

## 1. BACHEM (SIX: BANB)

**FY2025 actuals:** Net sales CHF 695.1m (CHF 605.3m prior year) = +14.8% reported, **+19.2% in local currencies**. EBITDA CHF 214.7m (margin 30.9%). Net profit CHF 148.8m (+24%).

**The ">CHF 1bn" question:** The skeptic claimed this is a medium-term ambition, not a 2026 target — this is **partly wrong**. Reputable reporting states Bachem "aims for annual sales of more than CHF 1bn and EBITDA margin over 30% **in 2026**." Mechanically consistent: CHF 695m × 1.45 ≈ CHF 1.0bn, so >CHF 1bn is the top end of the same 35–45% 2026 guidance, driven by the Building K ramp-up (first phase Swissmedic-inspected end-2025). It is an aspirational 2026 figure contingent on the upper end of guidance — not a separate, later "medium-term" target. The skeptic's broader point that 35–45% is extreme for a CDMO is valid context: press flagged single-facility (Building K) execution risk in this target.

*Informational structural analysis — not investment advice. As of 2026-05-31.*

The question this note answers is whether the **15 names are 15 bets, or a smaller number of bets wearing 15 tickers.** The conclusion, developed below, is the latter: the book is structurally closer to **4–6 independent bets**, and a large majority of it is a single stylistic wager.

---

## 1. Correlation-cluster map — what actually moves together, and why

The portfolio resolves into a small number of **return drivers**. The clusters below are ranked by how tightly the members co-move and how concentrated the weight is.

### Cluster A — The CSU/TOI serial-acquirer ecosystem (~36% if you include all VMS; ~21% in the controlled core)
**Constellation 12% + Topicus 9%** are not two bets. CSU owns **~30% of Topicus on a fully-diluted basis** and spun it out of its own TSS group — Topicus *is* the European limb of the Constellation playbook, run by overlapping people, capital-allocation philosophy, and culture. When the market re-rates the "buy-and-hold-forever vertical software roll-up" model, these two move as one. **Vitec 6%** is a deliberate Nordic clone of the identical model. **ATOSS 9%** is a different animal mechanically (single-product organic SaaS, not a roll-up) but trades on the *same investor narrative* — German-quality, high-FCF, premium-multiple software — so it shares the multiple-compression risk even if its operations don't.
- **Shared drivers:** software-multiple regime, "quality growth" factor, EUR/CAD software sentiment, rates (long-duration cash flows).
- **Tight sub-cluster (CSU+TOI):** ownership-linked, ~21%. Effectively one company for correlation purposes.
- **Whole-cluster weight: ~36%.**

### Cluster B — The bioprocessing / GLP-1 capex cycle (~15% core, ~24% with the adjacencies)
**Bachem 9% + Sartorius Stedim 6%** ride the *same single cycle*: the build-out and subsequent digestion of biologics/peptide manufacturing capacity. Bachem is a peptide CDMO (GLP-1 tailwind and overhang); Sartorius Stedim sells the bioprocessing consumables/equipment into the same end-customers. ## 2. Effective number of independent bets (vs. nominal 15)

**Approach.** The intuition: independent bets reduce risk like √N; correlated bets don't. A standard structural proxy is the **effective number of bets**

> N_eff ≈ 1 / Σ(cluster weight)²  *(a Herfindahl on independent risk clusters, the diversification analogue of HHI),*

applied **to the correlation clusters, not the line items**, because line items inside a cluster are near-redundant. I also sanity-check against a participation-ratio intuition (how the variance would actually spread if intra-cluster correlation ≈ 0.7–0.9 and cross-cluster ≈ 0.3–0.5).

**Step 1 — collapse to risk clusters (with judgment weights):**

| Cluster | Weight | Internal correlation |
|---|---|---|
| A. VMS / quality-software ecosystem | 36% | very high (CSU+TOI ~1.0; rest 0.7+) |
| B. Life-sci supply / bioprocessing | 24% | high within Bachem+Sartorius; moderate to E&Z/Bioventix |
| C. Holdco compounders | 18% | high (shared NAV-discount + private-market factor) |
| D. Compliance/sovereignty quality | 12% | low operationally, but style-linked to A |
| E. Emerging Asia | 10% | low vs. everything else (the real diversifier) |

**Step 2 — naïve HHI on these 5 clusters:**
Σw² = 0.36² + 0.24² + 0.18² + 0.12² + 0.10² = 0.1296 + 0.0576 + 0.0324 + 0.0144 + 0.0100 = **0.244**
→ N_eff ≈ 1 / 0.244 ≈ **4.1 clusters.**

**Step 3 — adjust for cross-cluster linkage (the overlays in §1).** Clusters A and D share the quality-growth style; A, B, C, D all share the EUR + long-duration-rate factor; B and C overlap on EU healthcare; C partly *is* private-growth. These positive cross-correlations mean the true N_eff is **below** the 4.1 the clean-cluster math implies. Pulling the developed-Europe-quality clusters partly together and leaving EM Asia (and, weakly, the US-listed defensives) as the only genuinely separate risk:

> **Effective independent bets ≈ 3 to 4, against a nominal 15.**

So the line-item count overstates diversification by roughly **4x**. You are paying the monitoring/complexity cost of 15 positions for the risk-spreading of ~3–4. Stated differently: of the "15," roughly **two are duplicates by ownership (CSU/TOI)**, **two more are duplicates by cycle (Bachem/Sartorius)**, **two-plus are duplicates by factor inside the holdco sleeve (Investor AB/Sofina private-markets)**, and the holdcos double-count the healthcare you already own outright.

*Assumptions shown: cluster definitions and the high/low correlation buckets are structural judgments, not estimated from a return series. The exact N_eff would shift with a real covariance matrix, but the order of magnitude — single digits, not low-teens — is robust to reasonable changes in those assumptions.*

## 3. The single-bet framing — how much is one wager?

Strip the labels and ask what *thesis* each holding expresses. The dominant one is:

> **"High-quality, high-return-on-capital compounders, bought at premium multiples, will keep compounding and keep their premium."**

Map the weights onto that single sentence:

- VMS ecosystem (A): **36%** — textbook expression of it.
- Holdco compounders (C): **18%** — same thesis, wrapped in a NAV discount.
- Compliance/sovereignty quality (D): **12%** — same premium-quality-compounder style.
- The high-multiple life-sci quality names (Sartorius, Bioventix, arguably Bachem): **~15% of the book** — again premium quality-growth.

That is roughly **70–80% of the portfolio expressing one stylistic wager**: *quality-compounding, in favour, at a premium multiple, financed by low long-term real rates.* The single biggest risk to the book is therefore **not** any company failing — it is the **style going out of favour** (a 2022-type quality/duration de-rating, or a sustained value/cyclical/rates regime), which would mark down ~three-quarters of the holdings *through the same mechanism, at the same time,* irrespective of business performance.

The genuinely off-thesis weight is small: **EM Asia (10%)**, plus whatever portion of the defensives/EM is truly cyclical-or-value rather than quality-growth. Call it **~10–15% of the book that is *not* the one wager.**

**Bottom line of §3: this is ~75% one bet on a single factor, dressed as five buckets.**

## 4. What genuine ballast would do (scenarios, not recommendations)

The portfolio is **long quality-growth, long duration, long Europe, long "things go right."** It has essentially **zero** of the asset types that historically *zig when this style zags*. The absent ballast and what each would structurally change:

**Absent entirely:**
- **Energy / real assets / broad commodities** — the natural hedge against the inflation/rates regime that is *precisely* what de-rates this book.
- **US mega-cap tech / US large-cap broad index** — the portfolio is conspicuously light on the world's largest profit pool and the USD; its "tech" is all European/Canadian small-mid software.
- **Cash / short-duration bonds** — no dry powder, no negative-beta, no convexity to buy the drawdown.
- **Gold / gold miners** — no monetary-debasement or tail hedge.
- **Broad value / cyclical equity** — nothing that *benefits* from the style rotation the book is most exposed to.

**Scenario sketches (qualitative, directional):**

- *Scenario 1 — rates/quality-growth de-rating (2022 redux).* Today: ~75% of the book marks down together through the duration channel; EM Asia (10%) provides thin offset; expect a deep, broad drawdown with little internal dispersion to cushion it. *With 10–20% ballast* (energy + short bonds + gold): the ballast is flat-to-up while equities fall, mechanically shaving the peak-to-trough by roughly its weight-times-its-divergence — turning a, say, ~35% style drawdown into something materially shallower, and crucially giving **rebalancing fuel** to buy the compounders cheap.
- *Scenario 2 — Europe-specific shock (energy, political, EUR stress).* Today: ~55–65% EUR exposure means the book takes it on the chin with few non-European offsets. *With ballast* skewed to USD assets (US tech/index, USD cash): currency and geography diversification directly dampen the hit.
- *Scenario 3 — inflation/cyclical-value regime persists for years.* Today: the book *structurally underperforms* for an extended stretch — not a crash, a grind — because its single factor is out of favour. *With ballast* in energy/real assets/value: a meaningful sleeve actively *earns* in that regime, converting a multi-year drag into something closer to flat.
- *Scenario 4 — risk-on, quality-growth rips.* Today: the book screams higher (this is the upside the concentration is buying). *With 10–20% ballast:* you give up some of that upside — that is the honest cost. Ballast is insurance; in the good state it is a drag.

The trade is explicit: **ballast lowers the ceiling a little to raise the floor a lot and to add rebalancing optionality.** Whether that trade is worth it depends on the holder's tolerance for a deep, *correlated* drawdown and a possible multi-year style winter — not on whether any company is good.

---

## 5. Blunt bottom-line — diversified or diversified-looking?

**Diversified-looking.**

- It *reads* as 15 names across 5 themes and 3 continents.
- It *behaves* as **~3–4 independent bets**, **~75% of which is a single stylistic wager** on premium-multiple quality-compounding in a benign-rates world.
- The diversification it advertises is mostly **cosmetic redundancy**: CSU/TOI are one bet by ownership (~21%); Bachem/Sartorius are one bet by cycle (~15%); the three holdcos collapse onto one NAV-discount + private-markets factor *and* double-count healthcare you already own; and ~55–65% of everything is the same EUR/long-duration trade.
- The only **genuine** diversifiers are the **~10% EM Asia sleeve** and, partially, the US-listed defensive Veralto — and they are the smallest, lowest-conviction parts of the book.

This is a **high-conviction, well-curated, single-factor portfolio.** That is a legitimate way to invest *if it is chosen on purpose* — but it should be understood as **concentration, not diversification.** The fifteen tickers are the *expression* of one view; they are not fifteen views. Anyone holding it should size it, and stress-test it, as the ~3–4-bet, ~75%-one-factor book that it actually is.

# AI vs Vertical-Market Software — Moat Tripwires

*Equity research note. Date: 2026-05-31. Portfolio context: Constellation Software (TSE: CSU), Topicus (TSXV: TOI), ATOSS Software (ETR: AOF), Vitec Software (STO: VIT-B) — ~36% combined. CSU controls TOI (~21% of book is one CSU/TOI ecosystem; treat correlated, not diversified).*

**Central question:** Does generative AI erode the moats of vertical-market / serial-acquirer software businesses?

---

## 1. Bull case vs. bear case (stated fairly)

### Bull case — why vertical, regulation-embedded systems-of-record resist AI

- **The moat is a stack, and AI hits the layers unevenly.** A useful decomposition (LongYield, 5i) splits the VMS moat into ~5 layers: (1) switching costs / data-migration pain, (2) regulatory-compliance embeddedness, (3) accumulated organizational/workflow knowledge, (4) integration into mission-critical operations, and (5) feature completeness. AI compresses development cycles (3–5 years → 12–18 months), so it attacks **layer 5 (feature parity)** hardest — but layers 1–3 are "fundamentally immune": no model instantly migrates decades of institutional data or assumes regulatory continuity.
- **Mission-critical, low-ARPU, fragmented niches are unattractive to AI-native attackers.** These are tiny TAMs (a software product for, e.g., funeral homes, dental labs, municipal water utilities, Nordic property management). The economics that protected them from horizontal SaaS protect them from AI startups too: the prize is too small, the data too specialized, the buyer too conservative.
- **Customers are conservative and slow.** ATOSS and Vitec management both describe customers wanting benefits "without major disruptions" and adopting gradually. European / government / regulated buyers move slowly — Topicus's base skews exactly this way.
- **Proprietary, workflow-embedded data is the AI *advantage*, not the vulnerability.** The incumbent that owns the system of record can layer AI on top (forecasting, automation) and deepen lock-in. ATOSS already sells AI forecasting as a *deciding factor* in hospital deals.
- **The serial-acquirer model is anti-fragile to single-vertical disruption.** CSU/Topicus hold hundreds of independent businesses across hundreds of niches. Idiosyncratic disruption in any one is diversified away, and the capital-allocation engine can redeploy FCF regardless of which verticals win. Topicus is "not just a software company; it's a capital allocator."
- **Pricing power is being defended deliberately.** Vitec is explicitly re-architecting pricing toward *value-based* models so that customer efficiency gains from AI don't translate into seat/volume revenue loss — "fair compensation for the value provided, regardless of efficiency gains."

### Bear case — where AI genuinely threatens

- **Per-seat licensing is structurally exposed.** The broad 2026 software re-rating (the "SaaS reset" / "SaaSpocalypse") is built on agentic AI cannibalizing per-seat revenue. Several of these businesses still bill on users/seats. As headcount at customers flattens or falls, seat-based recurring revenue can erode even with zero logo churn.
- **System-of-record primacy is contested.** The a16z thesis: AI collapses the distance between intent and execution, turning systems of record into a "commodity persistence tier" beneath an agent layer. If the *interface* and *workflow logic* migrate to an AI layer, the incumbent's UI/feature moat thins even where its database survives.
- **Feature-parity barriers fall fast.** The very thing that let small VMS vendors out-execute generic competitors — deep, niche feature sets built over years — can now be replicated in 12–18 months by an AI-accelerated competitor. LongYield/5i estimate **25–35% of CSU's portfolio faces meaningful AI-native disruption risk within 5 years**.
- **Pricing/index headwinds are already visible at the most price-driven name.** Vitec's organic recurring growth is guided *down* (FY25 ~3–4% → Q1'26 guide ~2–3%) on weaker index-linked price escalators — not AI per se, but it removes the cushion that masks any future volume softness.
- **Maintenance/legacy lines are in managed decline.** Perpetual-license and on-prem maintenance lines are shrinking by design (cloud transition) — fine when cloud growth more than offsets, but it narrows the margin for error if cloud growth ever stalls.

**Synthesis:** Bull and bear are not mutually exclusive. The honest framing is *which layer, which vertical, which revenue model*. Regulated systems-of-record with non-seat pricing and sticky data are well-defended; seat-priced, feature-differentiated, low-regulation niches are the soft underbelly. The portfolio sits closer to the defended end — but is not immune, and the cushions are thinning.

1. **Lag risk.** Switching costs mean erosion would appear with a *multi-year lag* — renewals are sticky, so today's healthy recurring lines could mask attrition that only surfaces at the next 3–5 year contract cycle. Current green prints are *consistent with* but do not *prove* durable moats.

3. **The 25–35% exposed slice (CSU/TOI) is real but unmeasured at the line-item level** — blended organic can stay positive while a quarter of the portfolio slowly bleeds, masked by the healthy 65–75%. The blended number is reassuring but not granular.

**Bottom line for a concentrated holder:** The thesis is currently *supported* by the fundamentals — these are among the better-defended corners of software (regulated, niche, sticky data, conservative buyers, non-trivial non-seat revenue, and — for CSU/TOI — a capital engine that diversifies single-vertical risk). **Stay long, but instrument the position with the Section 3 tripwires.** The thesis does not flip on narrative or share-price drawdowns; it flips on **T1 going red and being corroborated by churn/NRR or a competitive-loss narrative.** Until then, treat 2026 as a monitoring year, with Vitec's pricing/recurring line and CSU/TOI's maintenance-organic line as the two gauges to watch every quarter.
