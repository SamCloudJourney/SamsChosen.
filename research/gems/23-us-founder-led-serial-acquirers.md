## Lane Brief

The archetype: Transdigm/Heico/Roper/Watsco/Constellation-DNA but **smaller, less-known**, operating in fragmented niches where a disciplined, owner-operator capital allocator can compound capital at 15–30%+ ROIC for a decade by buying local/niche businesses at 4–8x EBITDA and layering on operational improvement. Lane 04 (US small/mid compounders) already captured OTC Markets (OTCM), IBP, InterDigital, and Kinsale — so this lane hunts **new names not yet in the master index** while retaining IBP as a cross-lane corroborating signal.

## GEM 1: CSW INDUSTRIALS (CSW)

**Name | Ticker | Country | ~Mkt Cap | Stage**
CSW Industrials | CSW (NYSE) | USA | ~$4.5B | Established compounder, accelerating M&A phase

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early
CSW supplies niche HVAC/R, plumbing, and building-maintenance products — deeply embedded in the "infrastructure of buildings." Three structural tailwinds: (1) US HVAC/R replacement wave driven by aging infrastructure and refrigerant-transition (move from R-22 to R-454B and R-32 systems, requiring new fittings, valves, gauges — CSW makes all of these); (2) data-centre build-out driving demand for condensate management, access solutions, and HVAC filtration products; (3) building-energy-code tightening (IECC upgrades require better sealing/filtration — Contractor Solutions segment). Under-covered because: crossed $1B revenue only in fiscal 2026; thinly followed at sub-$5B; classified as "industrial products" — obscures the recurring, contractor-purchased nature of consumables (85%+ of revenue is non-discretionary maintenance/repair spend, not project-based).

### Moat
Three reinforcing layers:
1. **Brand + installer stickiness in contractor channels**: Products like RectorSeal (thread sealants), KOOL-IT (refrigerant equipment), and TRUaire (grilles/registers) are specified by name in contractor workflows — substitution risk low.
2. **Efficient-scale in specialty product niches**: Category leadership (e.g., No. 1 or 2 in HVAC sealants, drain-line products, access doors) means competitors can't profitably attack at scale.
3. **Acquisition platform**: M&A discipline compounding for 10 years; $1.65B deployed since 2015 IPO; $1B in last 12 months alone including Aspen Manufacturing ($313.5M, ~11x synergy-adjusted EBITDA) and Motors & Armatures Parts ($650M, ~10.5x synergy-adjusted EBITDA). Deal-flow advantage in mid-market HVAC/building products — known acquirer, cultural fit, no private equity overlay.

### Founder/Insider Ownership
- Joseph B. Armes — Chairman, President & CEO since 2015, former M&A attorney (Weil Gotshal). Not the original founder of any product, but effectively the architect of the CSW platform.
- Insider ownership: **~1.4% per recent data** (Armes sold 1,500 shares May 2026 at ~$199/share). Low for a compounder — this is a **flag** vs. peers like IBP (14%) or Winmark (17%). Armes is well-compensated through salary + equity grants but not a dominant holder.
- Source: stockanalysis.com statistics; SEC Form 4 filings (May 2026).

### Sourced Economics

NOTE: ROIC is diluted by recent large acquisitions (goodwill step-up); underlying ROIC on incremental deals likely 15–20%+ but hard to disentangle. - Source: StockAnalysis statistics (June 2026).

### 10-Year Bull Case + Asymmetric Math
Scenario: CSW compounds revenue at 10–12%/yr (mix of organic 4–5% + acquisitions); margin expands to 15–16% net; FCF margin reaches 15–17% as acquisition amortisation normalises. By 2034: ~$2.5–3B revenue x 16% FCF margin = ~$400–500M FCF. At 25x FCF -> **$10–12.5B cap vs ~$4.5B today = 2.2–2.8x.** In the bull case where HVAC/R transition accelerates (refrigerant changeover = mandatory equipment refresh) and data centres drive further condensate/filtration demand: 3–4x over 10 years plausible. No "10x from here" math — this is a steady 12–15% compounder.

### Top 3 Risks

2. **Low insider ownership (1.4%):** CEO is a professional allocator, not an owner-operator in the Constellation/IBP sense. Less skin in the game = greater agency risk.
3. **Valuation (34x P/FCF) leaves no margin of safety:** Any macro shock or acquisition miss will compress multiple sharply.

## GEM 2: WINMARK CORPORATION (WINA)

**Name | Ticker | Country | ~Mkt Cap | Stage**
Winmark Corporation | WINA (Nasdaq) | USA | ~$1.35B | Mature ultra-high-ROIC cash machine

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early
Winmark is a **pure-play franchisor of used-goods retail** (Play It Again Sports, Plato's Closet, Once Upon A Child, Style Encore, Music Go Round — 1,371 franchises across US/Canada as of June 2025). Structural wave: secondhand/resale retail is the fastest-growing retail category (ThredUp projects global resale market to $350B+ by 2027); Gen Z and Millennial embrace of circular economy normalises pre-owned across all categories. Winmark's model is zero-inventory, zero-capex: franchisees buy/sell used goods; Winmark collects royalties (~5% of sales) + franchise fees. Under-covered because: tiny float (~3.58M shares, ~$1.35B cap); thinly traded; boring, non-tech, consumer; most fund mandates too large or require liquidity. Essentially zero analyst coverage.

### Moat
- **Asset-light franchise flywheel:** Winmark has zero inventory, zero stores. It collects royalties on ~$2B+ of gross merchandise volume. Near-zero capex ($158K TTM). 96.7% gross margin. 62.8% operating margin. This is a toll road on resale retail.
- **Brand/network effects in used goods:** Play It Again Sports has 40-year brand history; Once Upon a Child is the recognised name in children's resale.
- **Switching costs:** Franchisees are long-term (avg tenure >10 years), trained in proprietary pricing software (IQ system for used-goods pricing), deeply integrated.
- **Over 2,800 available territories** as of June 2025: plenty of white-space growth remaining — not saturated. Source: Winmark 8-K June 2025.
- ROIC: **194.7%** (StockAnalysis, TTM). Note: Reflects negative equity base from buybacks + zero capex. True economic measure is FCF yield ~3.1% at current price and absolute FCF growth rate.

### Founder/Insider Ownership
- John L. Morgan: original turnaround architect (CEO 2000–2016), owns ~907,186 shares (~25.4% of float) worth ~$387M+. Now Chairman Emeritus / major holder. Source: GuruFocus insider profile.
- Current CEO Brett Heffes: running the business since 2016, maintaining the model.
- Total insider ownership: **~17%** (StockAnalysis). High.

### Sourced Economics
- **Revenue TTM:** $84.99M (pure royalty/fee revenue — not gross franchise system sales). Source: StockAnalysis.
- **Net income TTM:** $40.95M (48.2% net margin). Source: StockAnalysis.
- **FCF TTM:** $41.54M (48.9% FCF margin; capex only $158K). Source: StockAnalysis.
- **Operating margin:** 62.8%. **Gross margin:** 96.7%. Source: StockAnalysis.
- **Special dividend Dec 2025:** $10/share paid to shareholders. Source: Winmark 8-K Nov 2025.

- Source: StockAnalysis statistics (June 2026); GuruFocus.

### 10-Year Bull Case + Asymmetric Math
**Base case:** 1,371 franchises grow to ~1,700 over 10 years; royalty per-store rises with resale secular trend; FCF compounds at 7–10%/yr -> ~$80–90M FCF by 2034. At 28–30x -> **$2.2–2.7B cap vs ~$1.35B today = 1.6–2x + special dividends + buybacks.** Not a 10x. **Bull case:** resale goes mainstream across ALL demographics faster than expected; Winmark accelerates to 2,000+ franchises by 2032; royalty rate increases to 5.5–6%. FCF to $120M. At 30x -> **$3.6B = 2.7x.** Downside floor: used goods is counter-cyclical (consumers trade down in recessions). FCF yield of ~3% on a near-zero-capex business = very limited permanent capital loss risk.

### Top 3 Risks
1. **Growth ceiling / saturation:** Franchise openings average only ~40–60/yr; revenue growth is mid-single-digit. Not a high-growth compounder.
2. **Online resale competition:** ThredUp, Poshmark, eBay, Facebook Marketplace compete for secondhand-goods mindshare. Online platforms threaten in-store resale economics for higher-value clothing.
3. **No M&A reinvestment engine:** Growth is capped by franchise system growth. Capital is returned via dividends/buybacks rather than compounded through acquisitions. This limits the long-run ceiling.

### UK ISA Buyability
Nasdaq-listed common stock (ticker: WINA). **Buyable in UK T212 S&S ISA.** Standard US Nasdaq common stock.

### Portfolio Fit
- Not in any prior lane. Unique franchise compounder archetype.
- Closest comparison within the 15: **Bioventix** (tiny, high-ROIC, FCF machine, near-zero capex).
- Best replacement: **VinaCapital Vietnam** (4%) — Winmark fills same "high-quality, high-ROIC" slot with US exposure and a counter-cyclical twist.
- Could also be **"none — watchlist"** if the resale secular thesis isn't strong enough to displace an existing position. The lack of M&A compounding engine limits the upside ceiling.
- **Conviction: 3.5/5** — extraordinary economics and alignment, but limited upside ceiling and online resale competition risk. Better on pullback to 25x P/FCF (~$320/share).

## GEM 3: EVI INDUSTRIES (EVI)

**Name | Ticker | Country | ~Mkt Cap | Stage**
EVI Industries | EVI (NYSE American) | USA | ~$225M | Early-stage serial consolidator — highest asymmetry micro-cap

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early
EVI is building the **dominant consolidator of commercial laundry distribution and service** in North America. Structural waves: (1) $5B+ US commercial laundry market is highly fragmented — hundreds of independent distributors serving hotels, hospitals, healthcare, correctional facilities, and laundromats; (2) institutional/healthcare clients face tightening sanitation/ESG mandates driving equipment replacement cycles; (3) laundromat capex boom — private equity is consolidating coin-op laundromats at scale; all need equipment and service. Under-covered because: $225M cap, thinly traded (NYSE American), classified as a "distributor" masking the software + service overlay, and zero sell-side coverage. Henry Nahmad (CEO, age ~mid-30s) has followed the same playbook as Mark Leonard — most investors don't see it yet.

### Moat
- **Scale in a fragmented, relationship-driven market:** Commercial laundry equipment buyers — hospitals, hotels, correctional facilities — have long-term vendor relationships. EVI has acquired 31 of the best-run distributors in North America, retaining local relationships under its umbrella.
- **OEM relationships:** EVI holds exclusive or preferred distribution rights for major OEM brands (Pellerin Milnor, Continental/Girbau, Maytag Commercial). Switching OEM representation is complex and rare.
- **Service flywheel + switching costs:** EVI Connect (proprietary IoT platform) embeds into client equipment, providing uptime monitoring, predictive maintenance, energy analytics. 35% of revenue is recurring parts/service — stickiest segment.
- **Parts inventory moat:** EVI holds proprietary parts inventory that generic distributors can't match — critical for hospitals where laundry downtime = regulatory risk.
- **Integration technology:** New field service software has already improved average technician response times by 13%. Source: distributionstrategy.com, Sep 2025.

### Founder/Insider Ownership
- Henry M. Nahmad: acquired controlling interest in March 2015 via Symphony Service Corp; currently **33.7% direct ownership** (4.3M shares). Effectively controls the company. Source: GuruFocus insider profile; EVI press releases.
- Total insider ownership: **~59.5%** (StockAnalysis). Extraordinarily high for a public company.
- Henry is ~mid-30s, highly motivated, has never deviated from the acquisition-compounding model in 10 years.

### Sourced Economics

At 15x -> $3.6B EV = **13x from here in 10 years.** This is the "Vista Energy at $10" math — real business, founder-controlled, structural wave, genuine 10x potential. **Downside floor:** Nahmad controls 59%; hostile acquirer would need his approval; FCF is positive and growing; $390M revenue platform at 6% EBITDA = ~$23M EBITDA, worth 8–10x = $185–230M; net debt $44M -> floor equity ~$140–185M (~37–50% below current). ## GEM 4: LIMBACH HOLDINGS (LMB)

**Name | Ticker | Country | ~Mkt Cap | Stage**

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early
Limbach is a MEP (Mechanical, Electrical, Plumbing) solutions company pivoting from low-margin general-contractor work (GCO) to high-margin Owner Direct Relationships (ODR) — working directly with building owners (hospitals, universities, data centres, pharma) on mission-critical HVAC/electrical systems. Structural waves: (1) **data-centre buildout** — mission-critical MEP is the bottleneck; Limbach is building expertise here; (2) healthcare infrastructure ageing — US hospital systems face massive deferred maintenance obligations; (3) **building electrification** — mandates drive HVAC upgrades. Under-covered because: small-cap, in transformation, historically dismissed as a low-margin GCO contractor, no sell-side excitement. The ODR pivot has been executing silently.

### Moat
- **Mission-critical MEP expertise:** Not all MEP contractors can handle hospital OR HVAC, pharma cleanroom HVAC, or Tier 3/4 data-centre mechanical plants. Limbach has this certified expertise.
- **Sticky Owner Direct Relationships:** Once embedded as a sole-source MEP partner (multi-year service + maintenance contracts), switching costs are high — building owners don't risk operational disruption.
- **Acquisition engine expanding:** Pioneer Power acquisition (Q3 2025) adds electrical/power capabilities. More M&A expected as ODR model scales.

### Founder/Insider Ownership
- Not classically founder-led. CEO Charles Bacon III has led the ODR pivot since ~2020.
- **Insider ownership: 6.76%** (StockAnalysis). Meaningful but below the Constellation-style ideal.
- Transformation thesis is management-quality-driven rather than founder-ownership-driven.

### Sourced Economics

- **Net income TTM:** $33.2M (+26.5% YoY). Source: StockAnalysis.

Not zero. 4x upside / 0.5x downside = reasonable asymmetry.
