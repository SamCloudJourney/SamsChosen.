# Test, Measurement & Automated Test Equipment (ATE) / Burn-In / Reliability — Hidden Gems

_Lane 22. Informational research synthesis, not financial advice. Economics sourced from company filings, earnings releases, and aggregator data; unverified items flagged. As of 2026-06-01._

**Lane thesis:** Advanced semiconductors cannot be shipped blind. Every AI processor, HBM stack, SiC power device, and silicon photonics chip must be electrically stressed, thermally exercised, and defect-screened before it leaves the fab or OSAT. The test layer is structurally indispensable — and as chips grow more complex, test intensity per die rises disproportionately. The market is dominated at the ATE level (Advantest, Teradyne, ~$20B+ each) but has deep niche sub-markets in probe cards, burn-in, thermal test, and mask-writing equipment where small companies hold near-monopoly positions that Wall Street consistently mis-categorises.

**Why under-covered:** (1) "Boring" — described as maintenance supplies to a fabless ecosystem most investors ignore. (2) Small/mid-cap with limited analyst coverage. (3) Non-US venues (Stockholm) that US fund managers ignore. (4) Revenue can be lumpy, obscuring the structural flywheel from recurring consumables/services.

**Pre-screen eliminations (not profiled in detail):**

## GEM 1: MYCRONIC AB — the invisible gatekeeper of all photomasks

**Name:** Mycronic AB (publ)
**Ticker/venue:** MYCR — Nasdaq Stockholm (STO)
**Country:** Sweden
**~Mkt cap:** ~SEK 57.5B (~$5.4B) as of May 2026
**Stage:** Quality compounder with AI-driven re-acceleration

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early

Mycronic makes **laser mask writers** — the machines that write the master circuit patterns (photomasks) used by every chip-making facility and display panel manufacturer on earth. This is the absolute upstream chokepoint: before a photomask exists, before an EUV scanner can print a wafer, someone has to write the mask with a laser system at sub-nanometre precision. That "someone" is overwhelmingly Mycronic.

Two structural waves:

1. **Display mask writing (near-monopoly):** Mycronic holds ~100% market share in advanced large-area display mask writers (OLED, 8K LCD). Every advanced display panel depends on photomasks written by Mycronic machines. This is a quiet, durable franchise with no credible competitor at the production-grade level.

2. **Semiconductor mask writing (SLX — growing):** The SLX series are laser mask writers for mid-to-leading-edge semiconductor photomasks. AI chip complexity = more mask layers = more mask writer demand. The SLX e2 series pushes into finer resolution, expanding the addressable market. Multiple SLX orders announced in 2025-2026 from Asian mask shops.

**Why under-covered:** (1) Listed on Nasdaq Stockholm — US institutions and most UK retail investors never see it. (2) Categorised as an "electronics equipment" company alongside lower-margin SMT machine makers. (3) The Pattern Generators crown jewel (50%+ EBIT margin) is hidden inside a conglomerate with the lower-margin assembly business. (4) The real economics are only visible at the segment level — the 64% EBIT margin line in the Pattern Generators segment Q1 2026 is the key number most investors miss.

### Moat

- **Display mask writers: sole production-grade supplier.** Zero credible competition at scale. Installed base lock-in: each mask shop runs Mycronic hardware and cannot easily re-qualify a different platform (6-12 month process; impractical mid-generation).
- **SLX semiconductor writers:** competing against NuFlare (e-beam) on cost/throughput at the midrange nodes that still constitute the majority of mask market volume.
- **IP moat:** decades of laser-physics, vibration isolation, and precision-mechanics patents. Replicating the system from scratch requires 10-15 years of accumulated know-how.
- **Pattern Generators Q1 2026 EBIT margin: 64%.** That is the moat in a number.

### Founder / insider ownership

- No founder concentration. CEO Anders Lindqvist holds 0.018% — effectively zero.
- **Anchor investor: Bure Equity AB — 23%.** Bure is a Swedish listed investment company (mini-Investor AB), active owner model, long-term holding orientation. Functions as patient quasi-founder capital.
- SEB Investment Management 10%, Fourth AP Fund 8.2%. Institutions ~52%.
- **Insider ownership is the one weakness in this thesis.** No skin-in-the-game CEO; relies on Bure as a patient capital anchor and on Swedish corporate governance culture.

### 10-year bull case + asymmetric math

**Bull scenario:** Display holds ~100% share with moderate volume growth (OLED penetration, 8K ramp). SLX gains 15-20% market share in mid-tier semiconductor mask shops by 2030 → segment doubles. Assembly grows 5-8%/yr with recurring mix increase. Group revenue SEK 7.9B → SEK 14-16B by 2033. EPS roughly triples. At 28-35x P/E = SEK 670-980/share vs ~SEK 285 today → **3-4x over 10 years, ~12-15% CAGR.**

**Downside floor:** Display mask-writer monopoly + installed base aftermarket = SEK 3-4B in recurring/semi-recurring revenue that does not disappear. Net cash SEK 2.78B. Genuine floor at maybe 15-18x trough earnings → ~30-40% downside in a severe scenario.

**Asymmetric kicker:** If SLX gains outsized share in semiconductor mask market as AI proliferates more chip layers, revenue accelerates non-linearly while market still prices it as a display-equipment play. That is the genuine "early" angle.

### Top 3 risks

1. **Display cycle / OLED capex pause:** New display fabs deploy in lumps; a 12-18 month order drought causes significant revenue decline. Stock fell ~40% in 2022-2023 on order weakness.
2. **SLX competitive pressure:** NuFlare (Toshiba) defends the high-end; Chinese competitors may commoditise lower-end semiconductor mask writing over time.
3. **Assembly business drag:** PCB assembly equipment faces genuine competition from Fuji, Hanwha, ASM Pacific. Margin compression could obscure the Pattern Generators quality.

Replacing the India ETF adds a hard-technology monopoly with AI tailwinds and active-owner governance via Bure.

**Conviction: 3.5/5.** Excellent crown-jewel business hidden inside a conglomerate structure, Bure as patient capital anchor, genuine AI upside in SLX. Tempered by: lumpy equipment orders, no CEO skin-in-the-game, assembly segment drag, and near-fair valuation at 31x. **Best entry: on any display-capex pause that pulls the stock back to 22-25x P/E.**

## GEM 2: AEHR TEST SYSTEMS — the only wafer-level AI burn-in gatekeeper (watchlist)

**Name:** Aehr Test Systems, Inc.
**Ticker/venue:** AEHR — Nasdaq Capital Market
**Country:** USA (Fremont, California)
**~Mkt cap:** ~$2.9B (May 2026, ~$82/share)
**Stage:** Early-scale inflection — pivoting from SiC to AI/HPC burn-in; pre-consistent-profitability

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early

Burn-in stress-ages semiconductor devices to trigger infant-mortality failures before they reach the field. As chips grow more complex, burn-in becomes more — not less — critical:

- AI processors running LLM inference in a $10M data-centre rack cannot fail mid-computation — hyperscalers are demanding production burn-in for custom ASICs.
- HBM4 stacks with 16-layer dies: one defective die destroys the whole package — wafer-level burn-in catches defects before expensive assembly.
- SiC power semiconductors in EVs must survive 15+ years at extreme thermal stress — automotive qualification demands burn-in.

**Aehr's unique position:** The only company globally offering **both wafer-level burn-in (WLBI)** and **package-level burn-in (PPBI)** for AI processors, SiC, GaN, silicon photonics, and flash memory. The FOX platform WaferPak Contactor covers 300mm wafers; the Sonoma system covers package-level AI ASIC burn-in at ultra-high power. No other company has demonstrated production-grade 300mm WLBI at this scale.

**Why under-covered/early:** (1) Only $59M revenue in FY2025 — too small for institutional coverage. (2) Was a one-customer SiC/EV story that collapsed in 2023-24 when EV demand softened. Most investors wrote it off. (3) The pivot to AI ASIC burn-in is recent and not yet understood — the pattern is analogous to Aehr's early SiC ramp, but in a structurally larger market. (4) $2.9B market cap on $59M revenue (~33x P/S) looks absurd until you model the FY2027 ramp.

### Moat

- **WaferPak patent-protected technology:** FOX platform's full-wafer contactor is proprietary; Aehr holds patents on parallel WLBI. No competitor has demonstrated equivalent production-grade 300mm WLBI.
- **High barriers to customer entry:** Qualifying burn-in into a production flow requires 6-18 months of reliability data and process integration. Once qualified, switching is essentially impossible mid-generation.
- **Dual capability (WLBI + PPBI):** Unique flexibility across the product lifecycle.
- **Key production win (Feb 2026):** Production purchase order for Sonoma systems to burn-in next-generation AI ASIC processors from a lead hyperscale customer. A second AI ASIC customer is ramping with committed shipment forecasts into FY2027.
- **Consumables flywheel:** WaferPak contactors are replaced multiple times per year per machine. The installed base becomes a high-margin annuity.

### Founder / insider ownership

- **Founded 1977.** Rhea J. Posedel (founder) remains Non-Executive Independent Chairman, ~497K shares.
- **Gayn Erickson (CEO since Jan 2012):** ~386-411K shares (~1.3%). Recent selling at $70-71 in Apr 2026 for tax/diversification — has sold but retains meaningful position.
- Institutions ~53%. Not a concentrated founder/family structure.
- Flag: CEO selling into the rally. Modest insider alignment relative to portfolio preference for founder control.

## GEM 3: COHU — test-handler tollbooth in recovery (watchlist)

**Name:** Cohu, Inc.
**Ticker/venue:** COHU — Nasdaq
**Country:** USA (San Diego, California)
**~Mkt cap:** ~$2.5B (May 2026, ~$52.76/share)
**Stage:** Cyclical recovery — returning to growth after automotive/industrial correction

### Structural wave & why under-covered/early

Cohu is the **#1-to-#2 global supplier of semiconductor test handlers and test contactors** — the mechanical systems that pick, place, and thermally condition chips during electrical test at the back end. Every device test requires a handler to deliver the device to the tester (Advantest/Teradyne) and a contactor to make the electrical connection. Cohu makes both.

Three structural tailwinds:

1. **AI/HPC chip test complexity:** AI processors run hot, have thousands of I/O points, and require precise thermal conditioning. Cohu's Eclipse platform with T-Core Active Thermal Control (ATC) is uniquely qualified for next-generation HPC testing. $30M+ follow-on orders from two customers in 2026 for HPC-specific systems.
2. **Power semiconductor (SiC/GaN) test recovery:** EV and industrial power device market corrected in 2023-24. SiC burn-in/test recovering as automotive qualification ramps resume. Cohu is market leader in thermal management for power semiconductor test.
3. **Installed base recurring engine:** ~25,000 Cohu systems at customer sites globally. Recurring revenue (spares, services, contactors) was ~$270M in 2025 — **60% of total revenue.** This base is capital-installed and grows with utilisation.

**Why under-covered:** (1) Currently loss-making on GAAP (operating loss $70M FY2025) — screeners eliminate it. (2) Confused with the 2022-2023 inventory correction; most sell-side has not updated the thesis for the HPC mix shift. (3) Only 8-10 sell-side analysts.

### Moat

- **Top-2 test handler market position:** ~20-25% global handler share. 25,000 installed systems across 7 countries.
- **Thermal management niche leadership:** T-Core ATC is the only handler-integrated active thermal control system qualified for HPC/GPU testing at extreme temperatures.
- **Qualification switching cost:** Handler + ATE + contactor = a qualified test cell. Switching vendors requires months of re-qualification. 25,000 sticky positions generating recurring revenue.
- **Test contactor consumable:** Replaced every 100K-500K insertions. One major device family = 6-figure annual contactor spend. High-margin, recurring.

### Founder / insider ownership

- No founder. CEO Luis Müller ~928K shares (~2%) — some recent selling (45K shares sold May 22, 2026).
- **0 buys, 9 insider sales in past 6 months** — yellow flag. Institutional ownership ~90%. No anchor shareholder.

## SUPPLEMENTAL: FORMFACTOR (FORM) — probe card leader, but fully discovered

FormFactor is the world's leading probe card supplier for DRAM and HBM testing. ~26% global probe card market share. SmartMatrix architecture is the only production-proven solution for HBM4's 16-high stacks and I/O speeds above 8 Gbps. All three HBM manufacturers (SK Hynix, Samsung, Micron) use FormFactor probe cards. Q1 2026 revenue was a record $226M (+32% YoY), Q2 2026 guidance $240M ± $5M.

**Why it fails the mandate:**
1. **Fully discovered and re-rated.** Market cap $9.7B. Stock +398% in one year. Trailing P/E ~77x.
2. **Heavy insider selling.** CEO Mike Slessor: 0 buys, 23 sells totalling $6M+ in past year. Multiple directors also selling.
3. **Memory cyclicality.** HBM probe cards = ~70% of DRAM revenue. When HBM capex pauses, probe card demand drops sharply — FormFactor went from $207M/quarter (Q3 FY2024) to $171M (Q1 FY2025) on slight memory softness.
4. **Not founder-aligned.** Professional CEO, large institutions. No anchor owner.

**Verdict:** Excellent quality business at peak cycle pricing with insider selling. Reject at current prices. Would re-evaluate at $40-50 (60-65% pullback) if HBM4 moat remains intact.
