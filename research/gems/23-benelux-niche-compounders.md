## Candidate 2 — Aalberts Industries

**Name:** Aalberts N.V.
**Ticker + venue:** AALB.AS — Euronext Amsterdam
**Country:** Netherlands
**~Market cap:** ~EUR 3.1B (stock ~EUR 32–36; all-time high EUR 59.76 in Jan 2022; currently ~40% off peak)
**Stage:** Mid-cap industrial compounder — cyclical trough, recovery inflecting Q1 2026

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Aalberts is picks-and-shovels into three structural waves simultaneously: decarbonisation (heat pumps, hydronic flow control for district heating/cooling), semiconductor precision (ultra-pure fluid systems + vibration isolation frames for ASML litho machines), and aerospace/industrial precision (surface treatment). Its three segments — Building (heat pumps/HVAC ~40% revenue), Industry (surface treatment, mechatronics), Semicon (ultra-pure fluid systems) — are all riding 10-year tailwinds. Why under-covered now: the stock has de-rated 40%+ from peak as EU housing slowed and Semicon disappointed in 2024–2025. Q1 2026 showed the inflection: +2.8% organic growth, EBITA margin recovering to 14.2% vs 13.2% full-year 2025. Recovery is beginning before consensus has re-engaged.

### Moat

- **Niche engineering lock-in:** 150+ operating companies in hyper-niche positions (e.g., ball-valve flow control for district heating). Once spec'd into a building or semiconductor fab, switching is disruptive and expensive.
- **Surface treatment (Industry):** World-leading heat treatment and surface coating for aerospace, auto, industrial — recipe-protected, geographically sticky.
- **Semicon mechatronics:** Ultra-precision vibration isolation frames and fluid systems for ASML/Nikon — requires years of co-development; not easily replicated.
- **Decentralised acquirer discipline:** 45+ years of bolt-on M&A. Management targets >18% ROIC by 2030 (vs current ~12.7% cyclically depressed). Jan Aalberts family holds 13.27% — long-horizon principal. 2030 M&A pipeline: EUR 800–1,000M additional revenue in NA + Semicon SE Asia.

### Founder / family ownership

## Candidate 3 — Melexis

**Name:** Melexis N.V.
**Ticker + venue:** MELE.BR — Euronext Brussels
**Country:** Belgium
**~Market cap:** ~EUR 2.4–3.2B (stock ~EUR 59–65)
**Stage:** Mid-cap automotive semiconductor — cyclical trough, structural EV tailwind

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Melexis designs sensor ICs for automotive (>90% of revenue): magnetic sensors, temperature, pressure, LIDAR drivers, current sensing, motor drivers. Every new car contains an average of 18 Melexis chips; EVs require 2–3x more Melexis chips than ICE vehicles (steer-by-wire, battery management, thermal management, motor control). The EV transition is a multi-decade volume multiplier. Why under-covered now: (1) automotive semiconductor cycle turned down sharply — revenue fell 10% in 2025 to EUR 839.6M, earnings -34%; (2) most generalists have sold it as an "auto" name without appreciating the EV content uplift per vehicle; (3) Xtrion (founder family entity) holds ~50% — very low float, limits index inclusion and institutional visibility.

### Moat

- **Design-in lock-in:** Automotive ICs are designed-in 3–5 years before production and remain on a platform for 5–10 years post-launch. Once spec'd, extremely difficult to replace mid-cycle.
- **IP ownership:** Melexis now owns 75% of IP of its product sales (vs 40% in 2006) — a measurable, strengthening advantage.
- **Automotive qualification barrier:** AEC-Q100 qualification requires 18+ months of reliability testing per chip. Structural barrier to entry.
- **Asset-light model:** Fabless (uses X-Fab, same Xtrion family) — high margins, low capex, high FCF conversion.

### Founder / family ownership

## Candidate 4 — Jensen Group (Jensen-Group NV)

**Name:** Jensen-Group N.V.
**Ticker + venue:** JEN.BR — Euronext Brussels
**Country:** Belgium (Danish founder origin)
**~Market cap:** ~EUR 600–740M (stock ~EUR 54–68)
**Stage:** Small-to-mid cap — inflecting strongly; record 2025 results; "Vista-pattern" candidate

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Jensen is the global #1 maker of industrial laundry automation systems: tunnel washers, ironers, sorters, conveyors — the complete machine-room for hospitals, hotel chains, prison laundries, rental textile businesses. Structural waves: (1) Labour scarcity — laundry is one of the most labour-intensive industrial processes; automation ROI is compelling as wages rise globally; (2) Healthcare hygiene — post-COVID hospital laundry specs have tightened globally; (3) AI/robotics disruption — Jensen's Inwatec partnership brings AI-based soiled linen sorting robots (THOR, ODIN, HEIMDAL) that handle contaminated linen without human exposure. Why under-covered: ~EUR 740M market cap, 1–2 analyst coverage, not in any major index, classified as generic "capital goods" when the automation/robotics angle is the real story. No equivalent publicly-listed peer globally (Kannegiesser/Girbau are private).

### Moat

- **Global market share #1:** Jensen holds ~18% of industrial laundry equipment market globally. No public competitor at this scale.
- **Switching costs:** A Jensen installation is designed into the building structure, integrated with building management, serviced by Jensen engineers. Replacement = shutting the laundry.
- **Consumables + service flywheel:** Post-acquisition of MAXI PRESS (2024) and G.A. Braun (2025 Americas), recurring consumables and service revenue is growing — higher-margin, more predictable than equipment alone.
- **Proprietary AI/robotics IP:** Inwatec AI-sorting algorithms + robotics platform creates a technology moat that pure-machinery competitors cannot replicate easily.
- **Geographic breadth:** Active in 22 countries, distribution in 50+ — scale advantage in service networks globally.
