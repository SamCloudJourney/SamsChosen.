## LANE THESIS

The chip-design toolchain and IP licensing stack is structurally indispensable and capital-light: every chip fabbed embeds licensed IP, and as chips grow more complex (AI SoCs, chiplets, sub-3nm, heterogeneous integration), the third-party IP content per chip increases. Business model: one-time license fee upfront → per-unit royalty for the 5–10 year life of every product that ships the IP. Gross margins 80–90%. Zero capex. Royalty backlog is invisible on today's P&L — exactly the "written-off, re-emerging" pattern sought.

**Market:** Semiconductor IP TAM ~$7.97B (2025) → $13.54B by 2030 at 11.1% CAGR (GlobeNewswire, March 2026).

**Important cross-lane note:** CEVA (Nasdaq: CEVA) and Arteris (Nasdaq: AIP) — the two primary picks in this space — were already discovered and fully detailed in **Lane 21 (Edge-AI Inference Silicon IP)**. Their appearance in this independent lane hunt constitutes **strong cross-lane corroboration** for both names. See lane 21-edge-ai-inference-silicon-ip.md for full sourced reports. ## PRIMARY NEW GEM — RAMBUS INC.

### Name: Rambus Inc.
**Ticker + Venue:** RMBS · Nasdaq
**Country:** US (San Jose, CA)
**Market Cap:** ~$16.4B (June 2026; ~108M shares × ~$151)
**Stage:** Profitable, scaling, product-revenue inflection; partly re-rated

### Structural wave + why under-covered

Rambus has transformed from a "patent troll" (1990s–2000s DRAM memory-interface litigation) into a genuine semiconductor IP + chip company. Two distinct revenue streams: (1) **Product revenue** — DDR5 Registering Clock Drivers (RCDs), Data Buffers, HBM4E memory controllers — physical chips sold to Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron for server DIMM modules; and (2) **Royalty/licensing** — patent licenses on memory interface protocols + Silicon IP licensing.

Why partially under-covered: Many investors still carry the "patent troll" mental model from a decade ago. The product pivot (+41% product revenue in FY2025) is not yet universally priced in. The upcoming **MRDIMM (Multiplexer Registered DIMM) transition** — full-scale adoption H2 2026 — increases silicon content per DIMM module, directly expanding Rambus's TAM. Management cites a $600M MRDIMM TAM. HBM4E memory controller IP ("industry's fastest" per Rambus Q1 2026 8-K) positions Rambus directly in the AI training cluster supply chain.

### Moat

1. **DDR5 RCD market leadership:** ~40% share in DDR5 Registering Clock Drivers — the critical interface chip every DDR5 server DIMM requires. Only 2–3 global qualified players; switching requires full JEDEC re-validation cycle (12–24 months).
2. **Memory IP patent annuity:** 25+ years of memory timing, signaling, and interface patents. Multi-year fixed licensing deals provide annuity revenue regardless of chip cycles. Q4 2025 licensing billings: $71.5M/quarter.
3. **HBM4E IP leadership:** "Industry's fastest HBM4E memory controller IP" — the spec needed for next-gen AI training clusters (GB300/Rubin-successor architectures).
4. **MRDIMM first-mover:** As dominant RCD supplier entering the MRDIMM transition, Rambus has validated silicon at the moment Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron need qualified suppliers immediately.
5. **Dual-revenue flywheel:** Product revenue (chips) and royalty/licensing revenue both scale with memory complexity growth — they compound together, not trade off against each other.

### Founder / Insider ownership

Not founder-led. CEO Luc Seraphin (since 2019) owns ~0.4% estimated. Total insider ownership ~0.8% (~$119M at $16.4B cap — management options, not founder equity). **Negative signal: 22 insider sales, 0 insider buys in past 12 months.** COO Xianzhi Fan sold $5.8M at $152. New CFO Sumeet Gagneja appointed April 2026 (transition flag). The case rests entirely on structural moat + business quality, not owner-operator alignment — a meaningful negative for the portfolio DNA.

### Valuation (June 2026)

- Market cap: ~$16.4B; net cash: ~$737M → EV ~$15.7B
- EV/EBITDA: ~31x; EV/FCF: ~28x (on ~$333M LTM FCF); ex-cash EV/FCF: ~22x
- P/E (TTM): ~46x; Forward P/E: ~33x
- FCF yield: ~2.0% on market cap
- Already a $16B company — "discovery" phase largely closed. Upside from here requires MRDIMM TAM expansion + HBM4 adoption to sustain 15%+ revenue CAGR to justify the multiple.

### 10-year bull case + asymmetric math

**Base case:** MRDIMM adoption ramps H2 2026. Silicon content per module increases (MRDIMM = more chips per DIMM). If 2027 product revenue reaches $500M at 37% op margin → $185M op income. Total revenue $900M–$1.0B by 2028, FCF $400M+ → 25x FCF = $10B EV... but that is below today's $16.4B unless growth reaccelerates to 20%+ CAGR, which requires HBM4/CXL 3.0 IP expansion.

**Asymmetric kicker (speculative):** Silicon IP licensing inflection where hyperscalers license Rambus IP for HBM5/CXL 3.0/next-gen protocols at enterprise fixed fees → royalty stream compounds independently of product cycle. [Flag: speculative — base case does not require it. Not verified in any current public disclosure.]

**The honest math at $16B:** On $320M LTM FCF growing at 15%/yr → $640M FCF by 2030 → at 25x = $16B. Already in the price. Need 20%+ CAGR to generate meaningful upside from here. The "hidden gem" window has largely closed.

### Top 3 risks

1. **Already partly discovered / expensive at $16B:** Market prices in MRDIMM/HBM4 success. Multiple compression from 33x to 20x fwd PE = 39% downside even if earnings hit targets — a genuine risk for a fresh-money entry.
2. **Customer concentration:** Revenue concentrated in Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron — a pricing war or NAND/HBM cycle turn would hit both product revenue AND royalties simultaneously.
3. **No founder alignment + systematic insider selling:** 22 insider sells / 0 insider buys in past 12 months is a clear caution flag regardless of business quality. Goes against the portfolio DNA.

### UK ISA buyability

**YES** — Nasdaq common stock. T212 buyable (US Nasdaq common stocks = standard ISA access).

### Portfolio fit

Given valuation concern (already $16B, discovery largely priced), this is a **watchlist / dip-entry** candidate rather than a buy-now pick. Re-evaluate at $100–110 (implies ~22x forward P/E ex-cash) for a 4/5 entry.

Could replace: **India ETF** (if memory IP exposure desired). But the insider-selling pattern and full-ish valuation make this less compelling than Arteris or CEVA at current prices.

**Conviction: 3/5** — excellent business with genuine moats (DDR5 RCD leadership, HBM4E IP, MRDIMM inflection), but valuation limits asymmetry for fresh-money position. Watch for dips.
