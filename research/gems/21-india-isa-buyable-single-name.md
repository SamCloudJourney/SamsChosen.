## The Core Problem with Lane 18

Lane 18 (India single-name compounders) identified four genuinely exceptional businesses — Polycab, CDSL, Neuland Labs, MCX — and then rendered the entire lane **practically useless** for this portfolio. Every pick trades on the **NSE/BSE** (ticker suffix .NS or .BO). NSE/BSE listings are **explicitly excluded** from a UK Trading 212 Stocks & Shares ISA. T212 does not support direct NSE/BSE access.

The ADR/GDR escape route is equally closed:
- **HDFC Bank ADR (HDB/NYSE):** Confirmed **NOT ISA eligible** by TISA — "depository shares will not be eligible for ISA inclusion because the underlying shares are not individually listed or traded on a recognised stock exchange in the EEA or UK."
- **Infosys ADR (INFY/NYSE):** Confirmed **NOT ISA eligible** by Hargreaves Lansdown — can be held in a SIPP or Fund and Share Account but **not a Stocks and Shares ISA or Lifetime ISA**.
- **ICICI Bank ADR (IBN/NYSE):** Already flagged in CLAUDE.md as "view only / NOT buyable."
- **Wipro ADR (WIT/NYSE):** ADR structure — expected ISA-ineligible on the same basis as HDB/INFY.
- **Dr. Reddy's ADR (RDY/NYSE):** ADR — same likely ISA-ineligibility applies.
- **LSE GDR listings (Reliance, Tata Steel, SBI, etc.):** 18 Indian companies have LSE GDRs; however, GDRs trade on the LSE's International Securities Market (ISM), which is **not a recognised stock exchange for ISA purposes** under HMRC rules — ISM listings are therefore typically ISA-ineligible. These are depositary receipts (not common shares), so they fail on the same logic as ADRs.

**Conclusion on direct India single-names:** There is genuinely NO cleanly buyable Indian common stock on NYSE, Nasdaq, LSE Main Market, or any T212-supported exchange. The India slot must be filled by a **UK-domiciled investment trust listed on the LSE Main Market**, which IS ISA-eligible as a company share.

## Candidate 1: ASHOKA INDIA EQUITY INVESTMENT TRUST

**Name:** Ashoka India Equity Investment Trust PLC
**Ticker + venue:** AIE.L (LSE Main Market, UK-domiciled investment trust)
**Country:** UK-listed trust investing in India
**~Mkt cap:** 384.4m GBP (421.6m GBP total assets)
**Stage:** Established/scaling — launched July 2018, FTSE 250 promoted June 2025

### Wave + Why Under-Covered
India's structural growth story (demographics, formalisation, digitalisation, infrastructure buildout) is well-known but the trust is under-covered: only a handful of analysts cover it. The trust is managed out of India/Singapore by White Oak, a firm that is largely invisible to generalist UK investors. The fee structure (no base fee, performance fee paid in AIE shares) aligns manager and owner in an unusual way.

### Moat
The moat is the **investment manager's information edge**, not the trust itself. White Oak Capital (45+ India-based analysts, ~$5.5B AUM) exploits structural inefficiencies in the Indian market — particularly in small and mid-cap stocks where analyst coverage is thin. Prashant Khemka spent 17 years at Goldman Sachs building India/EM funds managing up to $5B, then founded White Oak in 2017. The team has 30+ investment professionals based in India with genuine on-the-ground edge. The trust allocates ~43% to small-caps and ~50%+ to SMID combined — the zone of maximum inefficiency in India.

### Alignment
- **No base management fee** — White Oak is paid zero fixed fee; all compensation comes from a 30% performance fee on excess returns vs MSCI India IMI benchmark, measured over three-year discrete periods.
- Performance fee is paid in AIE shares (not cash), with a **three-year lock-up** on half those shares — manager is literally invested alongside shareholders.
- Ongoing charges (OCF excluding performance fee): just **0.20%** — cheaper than the iShares MSCI India UCITS ETF (0.65%) in non-performance periods.
- Prashant Khemka and White Oak co-invested at launch (amount unverified — flag).

### Valuation
Trading at -3.8% discount to NAV. NAV is a portfolio of Indian equities at roughly 22-24x trailing earnings. You are buying access to a demonstrably alpha-generating active manager at virtually no discount-bonus vs a passive ETF at 0.65%. The "fee risk" is if outperformance resumes — that is a good problem (you pay 30% of alpha, but bank 70%). In underperformance years, OCF is just 0.20%. The 30% performance fee is capped at 12% of time-weighted average adjusted net assets per three-year period.

### 10-Year Bull Case
India's economy likely 2-3x current size by 2036; equity market cap likely 2-3x in USD terms. White Oak's SMID tilt captures a disproportionate share: mid/small-cap India has historically compounded at 16-20% CAGR in INR terms. A 12-15% GBP NAV CAGR over 10 years implies a 3-4x return from current NAV. Discount could narrow to a persistent premium (FTSE 250 inclusion brings index buyers). Tax-free inside ISA amplifies compounding.

### Top 3 Risks
1. **India market re-rating risk.** The broad MSCI India index fell 6.6% in the year to June 2025, and -8.5% in the 12 months to May 2026. India entered 2025-26 expensive (25-28x P/E vs historical 18-20x). A genuine India bear market would hurt NAV regardless of alpha generation.
2. **Performance fee drag in good years.** In strong years, the 30% performance fee on alpha can be material — 16m GBP provisioned for the measurement period ending June 2027 on the back of strong 2021-23 returns. In the best scenario for shareholders (big outperformance), the fee is also large. Some investors find the fee structure psychologically difficult.
3. **Key-person risk — Prashant Khemka.** White Oak's competitive advantage is anchored in Khemka's process and team culture. If he departed or lost key analysts, the edge could erode. The trust has no direct recourse; if performance deteriorates for >3 years, investors can exit at NAV via ongoing share buybacks.

### UK-ISA Buyability
**CONFIRMED ISA-ELIGIBLE.** AIE is a UK-domiciled investment company (PLC) listed on the LSE Main Market. Shares in LSE Main Market-listed PLCs are fully ISA-qualifying under HMRC rules. Available on HL, AJ Bell, ii, Fidelity, and almost certainly T212. No PRIIPs/KID issue — UK-incorporated companies are not PRIIPs products.

### Could it replace the India ETF? Why?
**YES — the strongest case to replace the India ETF.** The iShares MSCI India UCITS ETF charges 0.65% for passive exposure and gives you no alpha, no manager alignment, and a market-cap-weighted portfolio dominated by Reliance, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and TCS. Ashoka charges 0.20% OCF (in non-outperformance years) and has delivered +82.6ppt of alpha vs the benchmark since inception. The SMID tilt has historically been rewarded in India. The "replaces India ETF" case is clear and compelling.

**Conviction: 4.5/5.** The only trust with a genuine, documented, substantial alpha track record. Fee structure genuinely aligns interests. Primary risk is Indian market valuation and key-person. At a 4% discount (near par), you are not paying for the alpha track record up front.

## Candidate 2: INDIA CAPITAL GROWTH FUND

**Ticker + venue:** IGC.L (LSE Main Market — Guernsey-incorporated investment company; ISA-eligible via LSE Main Market listing — see note)

**~Mkt cap:** ~83m GBP (118.1m GBP total assets at 28/05/26)
**Stage:** Established / turnaround — launched December 2005, managed by River Global (formerly Ocean Dial)

### Wave + Why
Specialist small/mid-cap India fund managed by Gaurav Narain, 30+ year veteran of Indian markets. Small/mid cap India has delivered outsized returns historically. The fund is genuinely overlooked — tiny analyst following, 118m GBP AUM.

### Moat (Manager)
River Global / Gaurav Narain edge: feet-on-the-ground Mumbai team, concentrated 39-stock portfolio, quality + valuation discipline. ### 10-Year Bull Case
Discount narrows from -12% to near-par as performance recovers. India mid/small caps continue re-rating. NAV grows at 13-15% GBP CAGR. Combined: 3-4x over 10 years. The Neuland/Federal Bank/Skipper portfolio is high quality; if Lane 18 picks were your target and you cannot buy them directly, IGC is an ISA-accessible proxy basket.

### Top 3 Risks
1. **Continuation risk.** Fund failed its three-year benchmark test in 2024 (lagged BSE Midcap by 9.6ppt). Board proposed changing biennial redemption to a performance-linked five-yearly tender (EGM March 2026). If shareholders reject or performance lags again, discount widens or wind-up becomes possible.
2. **Benchmark underperformance.** Avoiding PSU/real-estate/energy stocks (which drove 2024 gains) is principled but produces tracking error. In a broad India rally, IGC can lag.
3. **High OCF (1.62%) and manager transition risk.** The River Global acquisition of Ocean Dial introduces key-person risk around Gaurav Narain. At 83m GBP market cap, fund is vulnerable to a size death-spiral if discount widens.

### Could it replace the India ETF?
**Yes, but weakly.** Provides ISA-accessible India SMID exposure and a -12% discount offers some protection. The 5-year performance is genuinely impressive. However: OCF of 1.62% vs ETF 0.65% is a meaningful headwind; manager transition unproven; recent benchmark underperformance (2024) is concerning. Better as a satellite or at -20%+ discount.

**Conviction: 3/5.** Interesting SMID vehicle with real track record, currently hampered by OCF, manager uncertainty, and fresh benchmark underperformance.
