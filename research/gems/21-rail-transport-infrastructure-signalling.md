## Structural wave & why this lane is mis-priced

Rail is experiencing a once-in-a-generation capital cycle driven by three intersecting forces:

1. **European Green Deal / modal shift**: EU policy mandates doubling freight on rail by 2050 and tripling high-speed passenger capacity. Combined with TEN-T corridor requirements, every EU member state is in a multi-decade investment cycle. EU railway investment estimated at EUR500bn+ through 2030.
2. **ETCS / ERTMS digital signalling mandate**: The EU requires ETCS Level 2 on all TEN-T core corridors by 2030 and the extended network by 2040. Deployment costs run EUR500k to EUR2m per track-km. ~150,000 km of network needs upgrading -- a structural non-discretionary spend wave spanning 15+ years.
3. **Rolling-stock replacement + electrification**: Fleet ages averaging 25+ years in Europe; urban metro/tram expansion globally; hydrogen and battery rolling stock as a new S-curve.

Why under-covered: The rail supply chain is perceived as slow, cyclical, and "boring." Yet the aftermarket/lifecycle component of the best names is structurally recurring, margin-expansive, and largely invisible to generalist investors who look at rail OEM P&Ls (lumpy project revenue, low net margins) and miss the embedded recurring streams.

### Wave + why under-covered / early

KBX is the global monopoly in rail braking systems -- spec'd into ~85-90% of trains manufactured worldwide (estimated; company does not publish share officially). The aftermarket moat is self-reinforcing: once a brake system is qualified on a platform, it stays for 30-40 years of the train's life. Aftermarket grows regardless of new-build cycles.

The "early" element: KBX is in the middle of its BOOST margin-expansion programme (target: mid-teens EBIT margin by 2026, already hit early in Rail at 16.5-17%). The market has not fully re-rated for this trajectory. Potential structural optionality in separating the underperforming Commercial Vehicle Systems (CVS, truck braking -- declining market) from the structurally superior Rail Vehicle Systems (RVS) division. A CVS separation/spin is not announced but is logically compelling; if it happens, the rail pureplay would trade at a significant premium vs current blended valuation.

Additionally, KBX's 2024 acquisition of KB Signaling (train control/ETCS systems, formerly an Alstom unit) and integration of duagon (rail electronics) moves the company up the value chain from braking into the ETCS/ATO signalling stack -- a substantially higher-revenue-per-train opportunity. This is under-modelled by most sell-side.

### Moat

- Safety-certification lock-in: Every new brake design requires re-certification (SIL 4, TSI compliance). Qualification cycles 3-5 years. Switching costs prohibitive during a train's life.
- Global aftermarket infrastructure: Dedicated RailServices unit with parts, diagnostics, and maintenance contracts generating multi-decade recurring revenue. ~55% of rail revenues estimated to be aftermarket [unverified].
- Scale barrier: 200+ country service footprint economically unreplicable by new entrants.
- Signalling integration: KB Signaling positions KBX as a credible ETCS supplier alongside Siemens/Alstom/Wabtec -- a new revenue stream that rail operators increasingly want from a single integrated safety-systems provider.

### Founder / insider ownership

Thiele family holds ~59% via KB Holding GmbH and a family foundation (Heinz Hermann Thiele died 2021; transfer to a family foundation is underway). This is the strongest family-controlled anchor in any large-cap European industrial. Foundation structure ensures strategic continuity without quarterly short-termism.

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Vossloh is the global leader in rail fastening systems -- the elastic clips and plates that hold rails to sleepers. Without them, trains cannot run. Annual replacement cycles are driven by wear and regulatory requirements, not capex discretion. Estimated 30-40% global market share in fastening systems [unverified].

The "early" element: Vossloh completed the acquisition of Sateba (Europe's largest concrete sleeper manufacturer, acquired for EUR450m from TowerBrook Capital, finalised October 2025) in 2025. Sateba adds ~EUR500m revenue and dramatically expands the offering -- the company can now supply the complete permanent way: fastenings + sleepers + switch systems + digital lifecycle services. The 2026 revenue guidance of EUR1.56-1.66bn (+20% YoY) is the first full year of Sateba consolidation. Order backlog hit a new all-time high of EUR1.14bn in Q1 2026, +23% YoY.

Under-covered: ~10 equity analysts follow the stock; minimal US buy-side ownership; German small-cap = off most radars.

### Moat

- Mission-critical specification with lifecycle lock-in: Rail fastening systems are subject to lengthy homologation. Once spec'd into a line, the standard for 20-30 years is the same product.
- Guaranteed aftermarket replacement cycle: Fastenings wear on a predictable schedule and must be replaced -- virtually guaranteed repeat-purchase independent of new-build activity.
- Network and service footprint: 24-country manufacturing and service presence required by most national operators for compliance and emergency response.
- Sateba integration: Combining fastening systems + concrete sleepers creates cross-selling synergies and "whole-track" bidding leverage that competitors (Pandrol, Delkor) cannot replicate.
