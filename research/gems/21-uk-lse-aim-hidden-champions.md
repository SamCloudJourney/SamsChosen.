## Gem 1 — Cerillion PLC

**Name:** Cerillion PLC
**Ticker + venue:** CER — AIM (London)
**Country:** United Kingdom
**~Market cap:** ~£500M (shares ~1,300p; ~29.5M shares in issue; as of late-May 2026)
**Stage:** Established compounder, mid-cycle re-rating opportunity

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Telecoms globally are being forced to rip-and-replace legacy monolithic billing stacks (some 30+ years old) as they launch 5G, MVNO, and IoT business lines that require real-time, cloud-native charging. The BSS/OSS (business support / operations support systems) transformation market is a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar structural wave. Cerillion sells the mission-critical billing, charging, and CRM software that sits at the centre of a telecom operator's revenue engine.

Under-coverage reason: only 3-4 sell-side analysts cover it; it is too small for most institutional mandates; it occupies a specialist B2B software niche that mainstream investors rarely scan.

### Moat

- **Deep switching costs:** telecom billing is the most deeply embedded enterprise software after ERP. A rip-and-replace typically takes 18-36 months and costs 5-10x the licence fee in internal disruption — operators almost never switch once live.
- **Pre-integrated suite advantage:** Cerillion's BSS + OSS + CRM modules are pre-built to work together; competitors (Amdocs, Netcracker, Comverse) force expensive bespoke integration. For Tier-2/3 operators (Cerillion's sweet spot) this is decisive.
- **25-year domain expertise:** library of telco-specific data models, regulatory compliance modules, and proven migrations — impossible to replicate quickly.
- **Estimated ~5% share of a large addressable market** (source: GuruFocus FY2025 call) — enormous runway even at current trajectory.

### Founder / insider alignment

CEO Louis Hall (age 57) founded/built Cerillion through its 2016 IPO spin-out from Sema Group. Following a May 2025 stake sale of £45.6M (10.3% of company), Hall **retains 20.1% of shares** — worth ~£100M at current prices — making him overwhelmingly the largest shareholder. This was his first sale since 2017 and is understandable portfolio diversification after 25 years; he remains deeply aligned. (Sources: Shares Magazine, Investing.com — confirmed.)

## Gem 2 — Spectra Systems Corporation

**Name:** Spectra Systems Corporation
**Ticker + venue:** SPSY — AIM (London)
**Country:** US-headquartered, AIM-listed (UK)
**~Market cap:** ~$95M / ~£75M (as of early 2026)
**Stage:** Early-growth compounder — structural wave just beginning

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Central banks globally are in a multi-decade cycle of banknote modernisation — upgrading security features every 7-12 years to stay ahead of counterfeiting. Spectra owns proprietary authentication technology (covert nano-particle-based optical signatures) embedded into banknotes and secure documents. It is also expanding into: (1) smartphone-based banknote authentication (government pilots in the Middle East — confirmed by management), (2) gaming security software (record margins), and (3) tax stamp / secure document authentication (new contracts in pipeline per 2025 results).

Under-coverage: AIM micro-cap at £75M, covered by fewer than 3 brokers, zero institutional analyst initiations from major houses. Classic "too small to matter" blind spot.

### Moat

- **Regulatory lock-in:** once a central bank embeds Spectra's covert authentication signal into a note, they cannot change the chemistry without replacing the entire note issue (billions of circulating notes). Lock-in is effectively permanent for the life of the substrate programme — typically 15-20 years.
- **Trade-secret / know-how barrier:** the proprietary optical nano-particle chemistry is protected more by know-how than patents (intentionally — makes it harder to reverse-engineer). Dr. Lawandy has been perfecting it since 1996.
- **De facto sole-source:** the company is so specialised that most central bank procurement is de facto sole-source once validated. Competition is nearly non-existent in its specific authentication layer.
- **Gaming software division:** proprietary anti-fraud software for online gaming — a separate moat in a different regulatory space.

### Founder / insider alignment

Dr. Nabil M. Lawandy has been Founder, CEO, and President since 1996. He holds ~8.1% of shares (Simply Wall St). Insiders in aggregate hold ~£14M of the ~£104M business. Institutional holders own ~46%. (Sources: Yahoo Finance, Simply Wall St — verified.)

## Gem 3 — Beeks Financial Cloud Group PLC

**Name:** Beeks Financial Cloud Group PLC
**Ticker + venue:** BKS — AIM (London)
**Country:** United Kingdom (Scotland-based)
**~Market cap:** ~£156M (late-2025 data)
**Stage:** Growth compounder — inflecting from infrastructure build to operating leverage

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Capital markets technology is migrating from on-premise hardware to low-latency cloud — but financial services has unique requirements (microsecond latency, regulatory data sovereignty, co-location at exchange matching engines) that generic hyperscalers cannot meet. Beeks has built the only cloud-native infrastructure specifically engineered for trading latency, sitting physically inside exchanges (NYSE, CME, Cboe, Deutsche Borse, Euronext) as an authorised "proximity cloud" provider. This is picks-and-shovels on the financialisation of cloud.

Why early: the "proximity cloud" model has only 3 authorised providers globally (per Beeks IR). Contract wins at new exchanges create durable recurring revenue that compounds. The company has been investing heavily for 3 years; operating leverage is now inflecting. Still a £156M micro-cap; most institutions cannot hold it.

### Moat

- **Physical location moat:** you cannot replicate being inside NYSE's data centre with nanosecond proximity to the matching engine; exchange access is contracted, exclusive, and physically fixed.
- **Mission-critical switching costs:** trading firms rely on sub-millisecond execution; moving cloud providers mid-operation risks execution quality, compliance, and P&L — effectively zero switching.
- **Network effect:** more trading firms in the same Beeks proximity cloud = better liquidity, lower latency, more analytics data — creates a flywheel as more exchanges join.
- **Analytics layer (mdPlay / stream2Cloud):** Beeks is building a data/analytics business on top of infrastructure — higher-margin, stickier layer difficult for pure infrastructure players to replicate.

## Gem 4 — Ashtead Technology Holdings PLC

**Name:** Ashtead Technology Holdings PLC
**Ticker + venue:** AT. — LSE Main Market
**Country:** United Kingdom (Aberdeen, Scotland)
**~Market cap:** ~£285M (late-2025/early-2026)
**Stage:** Established compounder — structural wave accelerating

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Offshore energy is in a supercycle: deepwater oil & gas (OPEX-driven) is at record activity, and offshore wind installation is growing exponentially — and both require the same subsea equipment (ROVs, survey instruments, inspection tools, cable-lay gear). Ashtead is the dominant provider of subsea equipment rental to the world's offshore energy contractors. Crucially, ~85% of its equipment fleet is fungible between oil/gas and renewables, giving it a unique hedge as the energy mix shifts. As offshore wind scale accelerates through 2030-2040, demand for subsea equipment rental inflects regardless of which fuel wins.

Why under-covered: post-2014 oil crash, any "offshore" label triggers value-trap memories. Ashtead Technology is fundamentally different — it rents equipment rather than owning rigs, has repeatable utilisation-based revenue, and ROIC well above cost of capital throughout cycles.

### Moat

- **Fleet scale and geographic density:** 30,000+ pieces of mission-critical subsea equipment across global hubs. Replicating this fleet takes a decade and hundreds of millions in capex.
- **Specialist know-how:** subsea technicians who can operate, maintain, and calibrate this equipment are scarce and trained over years. Staff retention is itself a moat.
- **Customer sticky relationships:** offshore contractors are under extreme time pressure (rig day-rates $500K+); sourcing reliable equipment at short notice is critical. Ashtead's fleet breadth and geographic network win on reliability, not just price.
- **Equipment fungibility between O&G and renewables:** 85% crossover gives structural resilience as renewables projects multiply.

### Founder / insider alignment

CEO Allan Pirie (not a founder — company has PE/IPO background, listed 2021). Insider ownership low at ~2.2% (~£6.3M value). However, insiders have been buying: Chairman William Shannon made a notable purchase in January 2026. Institutions hold 85%. (Sources: Simply Wall St, Yahoo Finance — verified.) Insider ownership is the weakest characteristic here; notable buying activity partially compensates.

## Gem 5 — Tristel PLC

**Name:** Tristel PLC
**Ticker + venue:** TSTL — AIM (London)
**Country:** United Kingdom (Snailwell, Cambridgeshire)
**~Market cap:** ~£183M (shares ~382p; ~48M shares; as of October-May 2026)
**Stage:** Mature compounder entering a step-change growth phase (FDA clearance just received)

### Wave + why under-covered / early

Hospitals globally are under increasing regulatory pressure to decontaminate reusable medical devices — particularly flexible endoscopes and ophthalmic instruments — to eliminate healthcare-associated infections. Chlorine dioxide (ClO2) is the most effective chemistry for high-level disinfection of heat-sensitive devices. Tristel has spent 20+ years developing, validating, and gaining regulatory clearance for its proprietary chlorine dioxide formulations across 40+ countries. The structural wave is hospital infection control becoming non-negotiable; the "early" angle is the US market just cracked open.

The FDA moment: The FDA granted clearance for Tristel OPH (ophthalmic probe decontamination) in late 2025 — Tristel's first US product clearance. The US hospital market is 8x the size of the UK. This is a genuine step-change event under-appreciated at the current £183M market cap. A further US product pipeline (endoscope decontamination) remains in FDA process.

Why under-covered: AIM healthcare, £183M cap, only 2-3 brokers covering. The FDA filing and first clearance happened quietly.

### Moat

- **Regulatory approval library:** 20 years of safety data, clinical publications, and approvals across 40+ countries. No competitor has followed this specific path — Tristel is the only company with CE-marked, FDA-cleared chlorine dioxide decontamination products. Entry requires 3-7 years of regulatory work per market.
- **Consumable model:** hospitals use Tristel wipes every decontamination cycle (multiple times per day per device). Revenue is highly recurring, low-unit-value, high-frequency.
- **Network effects via clinical evidence:** published hospital outcomes data create self-reinforcing credibility that compounds over time.
- **Device compatibility library:** Tristel has proven compatibility with hundreds of specific instrument models — a library competitors cannot shortcut.

### Founder / insider alignment

CEO Paul Swinney developed the proprietary chlorine dioxide formulation in 1993 and has led Tristel through its entire public journey (listed AIM 2005). He directly holds ~1.5% of shares. Institutional ownership is 80-86%. (Sources: Simply Wall St, Yahoo Finance — confirmed.)

Insider ownership is modest — the weakest characteristic. Swinney's 32-year commitment provides founder-aligned behaviour even if the equity stake is relatively small.
