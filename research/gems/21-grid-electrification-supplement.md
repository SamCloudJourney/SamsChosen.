## GEM A: PREFORMED LINE PRODUCTS (PLPC)
**Ticker:** PLPC | **Exchange:** NASDAQ | **Country:** USA | **~Mkt Cap:** ~$1.81B | **Stage:** Mature niche compounder, inflecting higher

### Wave & Why Under-Covered
PLP makes the "invisible hardware" that holds every overhead and underground power line in place: dead-ends, suspension clamps, vibration dampers, armor rods, splices, grounding products, cable accessories. Every new transmission tower, every underground cable pull, every substation requires PLP fittings. As grid capex compounds at 10-15%/yr globally, PLP gets a toll on every linear meter installed. At ~$1.8B market cap with 47% insider ownership, it sits below most institutional radar screens — fewer than 5 analysts cover it. It is the ultimate "hardware behind the electricity that powers everything" pick.

Additional tailwind: Fibre/comms markets account for ~30% of revenue, with telco fibre buildout (rural broadband + 5G densification) layering on top of the power grid wave. Net-zero offshore wind interconnectors require specialised submarine cable accessories — PLP is moving into that segment.

### Moat
- Proprietary hardware + qualification barriers: Utilities and contractors qualify PLP fittings against specific cable types/tensions/environments. Re-qualification to a competitor takes months and carries liability risk — once specified in a project, PLP is sticky.
- Global footprint / local presence: 40+ countries, local manufacturing (30+ production sites). Utilities often require local-sourced product for reliability and compliance.
- 72-year installed base (founded 1947): On a piece of hardware that, if it fails, can bring down an entire transmission span, utilities pay a premium for the known supplier.
- Project-spec lock-in: Once a project engineer specifies PLP product in drawings, substitution requires re-engineering sign-off.

### Founder / Insider Ownership
- Ruhlman family control is exceptional for a public company: Robert G. Ruhlman (Executive Chairman, President & CEO) and Randall Ruhlman together hold approximately 45%+ of shares (Robert ~22%, Randall ~23% per recent data). Total insider ownership: ~47.7% (confirmed).
- Barbara P. Ruhlman Revocable Trust: ~16% (separate count).
- This is effectively a founder-family controlled company masquerading as a public one — the same DNA as Constellation, Topicus, ATOSS.
- Institutional ownership: ~67.6%.

### 10-Year Bull Case & Asymmetric Math
- Grid capex compounds at 8-10%/yr globally for 10 years (IEA, Wood Mac, BNEF consensus).
- Bull path: Revenue grows from $670M to $1.5-2B by 2033; EBITDA margins recover to 14-16% on operating leverage.
- At 14x EV/EBITDA on $250M EBITDA = $3.5B EV — roughly 2x from today.
- Downside floor: Family owns 47%, won't sell cheap. Grid spend is infrastructure-driven, not discretionary. Business generates FCF even in downturns.
- Not a Vista-like 10x, but a steady 2-3x compounder with minimal downside floor.

### Top 3 Risks
1. ROIC mediocrity: 9.2% ROIC and falling gross margins (30.9% vs 32%+ peak) suggest the moat may be thinner than the franchise narrative implies. Commoditisation of simpler fittings is real.
2. Valuation at 36x forward PE: The stock needs perfect execution on the grid capex thesis. Any cyclical pause in utility capex = 30-40% drawdown.
3. US tariff risk: Steel/aluminum-intensive products; raw-material cost surges could compress margins if pricing lags.

### UK ISA Buyability

### Portfolio Fit / Swap Thesis
Fills the "US non-tech, real-economy infrastructure" blind spot. Could replace India ETF (passive, no moat, no alignment) or VinaCapital Vietnam. However, the elevated valuation and modest ROIC makes this a watchlist/starter position rather than a conviction add today.

**Conviction: 3/5** (rises to 3.5/5 on a 20-25% pullback to ~$120-130 range, implying ~25-27x forward PE).

## GEM B: MYR GROUP (MYRG)
**Ticker:** MYRG | **Exchange:** NASDAQ | **Country:** USA | **~Mkt Cap:** ~$2.7B | **Stage:** Specialty contractor, inflecting on grid/data-center wave

### Wave & Why Under-Covered
MYR Group is one of the top-3 US specialty electrical contractors, executing the physical work of grid modernisation — T&D line construction/upgrade, substation wiring, renewable interconnections, EV charging infrastructure, data-centre power fit-out. It is the picks-and-shovels company that actually builds what ABB, Hitachi Energy, and Eaton sell. ~60% of T&D revenue is on master service agreements (MSA) — utility-called, recurring, long-horizon work. Its backlog ($2.82B at end of 2025, +9.6% YoY) reflects signed utility commitments through 2027-28. Record 2025 revenue of $3.66B. Underappreciated because "construction contractor" screens poorly on capital-return metrics, hiding a high-quality franchise.

Under-coverage reason: Most quality-focused screens eliminate MYRG immediately (low net margins, low ROIC in absolute terms) without recognising the capital-light nature (negative working-capital MSA model) and the structural demand shift from discretionary construction to non-discretionary grid infrastructure.

### Moat
- Top-3 scale + national footprint: Utility MSAs require pre-qualification, bonding capacity, safety records, and geographic scale. Only 3-4 contractors can compete for the largest national accounts. This scale barrier keeps new entrants out.
- Skilled lineman workforce: The US faces a severe shortage of trained linemen. MYR has built apprenticeship programs and a reputation as the preferred employer. Workforce = true moat in a tight labour market.
- Long-cycle MSA structure: ~60% of T&D revenues are contracted MSAs — utilities call off work as needed. Not project work; embedded in utility OpEx/capex programs.
- Data-centre buildout: Rapidly growing segment — hyperscale data centres require complex, custom electrical fit-out that only a contractor of MYR's scale can deliver.

### Founder / Insider Ownership
- Insider ownership: ~2% (low — not a family-controlled business).
- Mitigant: The business's quality is structural (MSA model, backlog), not dependent on founder vision.

### 10-Year Bull Case & Asymmetric Math
- US grid capex compounds at 8-10%/yr through the early 2030s; MYRG captures a growing share.
- Bull: Revenue $3.7B to $7-8B by 2033.
- At 5% EBITDA margin on $7B = $350M EBITDA; 12x EV/EBITDA = $4.2B EV — roughly 1.5-2x from today.
- Aggressive case (8% margin at scale): $560M EBITDA at 12x = $6.7B — roughly 2.5x.
- Not a 10x — but a steady 2-3x with low downside (utility-mandated work doesn't stop easily).

### Top 3 Risks
1. Thin margins: 6-7% EBITDA margins leave little buffer if labour costs rise unexpectedly. A 200bp margin compression wipes out ~30% of EPS.
2. Cyclicality risk: Despite MSAs, if utilities cut capex in a rate-shock or regulatory reversal, backlog can decline sharply (as happened 2018-2020).
3. Labour / safety incidents: A major safety incident can trigger customer MSA termination and reputational damage.

### UK ISA Buyability
NASDAQ common stock. No ADR structure. Buyable in T212 ISA (standard NASDAQ common stock).

### Portfolio Fit / Swap Thesis
Fills the "US real-economy execution layer of the grid supercycle" gap. Less moaty than Powell Industries (lane 06) but the MSA model provides comparable visibility. Could replace India ETF or complement Powell as the "contractor" to Powell's "equipment."

**Conviction: 3/5** (better entry sub-$400, implying ~23x forward PE).

## GEM C: NEXANS SA (NEX.PA)
**Ticker:** NEX | **Exchange:** Euronext Paris | **Country:** France | **~Mkt Cap:** ~EU3.5B | **Stage:** Restructured pure-play, value-vs-growth tension

### Wave & Why Under-Covered
Nexans is the world's #2 HVDC cable maker (behind Prysmian), having completed a multi-year pivot from a diversified industrial cable company to a "pure-play electrification" company. It exited low-margin telecom/building cable segments, focused capex on submarine HVDC, and is now one of only 3-4 companies globally capable of delivering HVDC submarine cable systems end-to-end (manufacturing + cable-lay vessels). Yet it trades at EV/EBITDA of ~11.9x — a 30-35% discount to Prysmian despite comparable HVDC exposure, because Nexans is less well-known outside France, has no founder/family alignment, and the restructuring narrative is not yet fully trusted by the market.

HVDC backlog: EU7.7B at Dec 31, 2025 (including EU1.2B from the Great Sea Interconnector). Key project wins: RTE France HVDC framework >EU1B, Eastern Green Link 3 (Scotland), offshore wind connections across Northern Europe. Third cable-laying vessel (Nexans Electra) entering service mid-2026, expanding installation capacity.

### Moat
- Oligopoly with extreme capital and technology barriers: Building an HVDC extrusion facility + cable-laying vessel fleet takes 5-8 years and EU1-2B+ of capex. Only Prysmian, Nexans, NKT, and (to a lesser extent) LS Cable/Sumitomo can do it at scale. The oligopoly controls >75% of the Western HVDC cable market.
- Installation capability: The cable-lay vessel fleet (3 vessels post-Electra) creates a second moat layer — you can manufacture but not install without the fleet.
- Long-cycle contracts with utilities: 3-5 year project timelines; signed frameworks with RTE, National Grid, Elia, Statnett. Government-backed utilities with sovereign creditworthiness.
- Technology: Proprietary HVDC insulation compounds (PPL/XLPE); digital cable monitoring; cross-bonding and accessory integration expertise built over decades.

### Founder / Insider Ownership
- No family/founder alignment. Institutional ownership ~82.6%.
- Largest shareholder: Invexans Limited (Chilean industrial holding) ~14.2%. Bpifrance (French state) ~5.2%. Baillie Gifford ~7.9%.
- Management insider ownership: under 1%.
- Clear quality ding relative to the portfolio's DNA.

### 10-Year Bull Case & Asymmetric Math
- HVDC cable market: $9.7B in 2025 to $59.9B by 2034 (CAGR 24.3%, per GM Insights). Even at half that growth rate, a 5-6x market.
- Nexans holds ~20-25% of Western HVDC market.
- Bull: At EU15B HVDC market (2030 conservative), Nexans revenues of EU5-6B standard sales just from this segment.
- At 16% EBITDA margin on EU6B = EU960M EBITDA; at 15x EV/EBITDA = EU14.4B EV. Current EV ~EU4.5-5B. Bull case: 3x from here over 7-8 years.
- Multiple expansion alone: if Nexans closes the Prysmian gap from 12x to 17x while EBITDA doubles = 2.8x on multiple expansion alone, before earnings growth.
- Downside floor: EU7.7B signed backlog = 4+ years of revenue visibility.

### Top 3 Risks
1. No family/founder alignment: Professional management with no skin in the game. History of strategic pivots could recur.
2. Raw material cost volatility: Copper + polymer swings. A 30% copper spike can materially compress margins before pass-throughs kick in.
3. Project execution risk: Deepwater cable projects have narrow weather windows, complex permitting; if one major project delays, revenue recognition slides 6-12 months.

### UK ISA Buyability
Euronext Paris (EPA:NEX). Common equity on a main European exchange. Buyable in T212 ISA — Euronext Paris is a supported exchange. No PRIIPs/KID issue for French common stocks.

### Portfolio Fit / Swap Thesis
Nexans is the "value" version of the HVDC cable trade (vs NKT in lane 06, the "quality" version). A portfolio already owning NKT would have overlap; a portfolio without NKT might prefer Nexans for the valuation entry point.

Could replace VinaCapital Vietnam (small, frontier-market-dependent, limited structural moat) or sit alongside NKT as a "cable oligopoly pair" if conviction is high.

**Conviction: 3/5** (rises to 3.5/5 if pullback to 9-10x EV/EBITDA, implying ~EU3B EV. The no-alignment issue is structural and keeps conviction capped.)

## Cross-Lane Signal Check

- Nexans + NKT (lane 06): Two independent angles both identify the HVDC cable oligopoly as a structural compounder. NKT is the quality pick (ROCE target >22% by 2030, family-institutional alignment, Copenhagen-listed); Nexans is the cheaper alternative with more execution risk. If NKT is owned, Nexans becomes redundant; if NKT is not owned, Nexans is the cheaper entry to the same wave.
- MYR Group + Quanta Services: The specialty electrical contractor theme appears across multiple grid analyses. Quanta (~$50B cap) is the larger name; MYR is smaller, less-covered, with comparable MSA economics.
- PLP + Powell Industries (lane 06): Both are "invisible hardware" plays on grid capex. Powell = custom switchgear (high-margin, moatier, 4/5); PLP = hardware fittings (lower-margin, broader/global, 3/5). Powell is the stronger pick; PLP is complementary diversification.
