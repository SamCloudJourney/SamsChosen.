## Candidate 1: Energy Recovery, Inc. (ERII)

**Name | Ticker+Venue | Country | ~Mkt cap | Stage**
Energy Recovery, Inc. | ERII, Nasdaq | USA | ~$421M | Profitable niche monopoly; transient air pocket in 2025-26

### Wave + Why Under-Covered / Early
Global freshwater stress is structuring a permanent desalination buildout: the desalination equipment market is projected to reach ~$49.8B by 2032 (CAGR ~8.7%), driven by Gulf megaprojects, water-stressed Sun Belt US, and the Indo-Pacific. ERII owns one asset: the Pressure Exchanger (PX), a ceramic rotary device that recovers 95-99% of the hydraulic energy in RO brine -- without a PX, seawater desalination is ~30-40% more energy-intensive, making large plants economically unviable. Market share in the SWRO PX segment is estimated at >90%. The stock is under-covered (8-10 analysts, consensus "Hold") because: (a) ERII killed its CO2-refrigeration pivot in 2025 after years of capex burn, triggering a 30%+ drawdown and institutional de-risking; (b) the core desalination book is lumpy (project delays = revenue air pocket in 2025-26); (c) the company's ~$135M revenue puts it below most institutional mandates. The crowd is pricing it as a broken story. The thesis: the pivot is over, cash is rebuilding, and the next major desalination capex wave (2027-2030) returns ERII to its structural growth path with a cleaner capital allocation story.

### Moat
- Near-monopoly in SWRO Pressure Exchangers (estimated >90% global share, confirmed by multiple sell-side sources). Ceramic device is proprietary; ceramic-forming knowhow is ~20 years deep.
- Switching cost: PX is physically embedded in plant design; replacing it means re-engineering the entire energy-recovery loop. No known equivalent product from a credible rival at scale.
- Certifications/testing requirements for large municipal plants are multi-year -- a barrier to greenfield entry.
- Next-generation PX Q650 (launched March 2026) offers 63% higher peak flow vs prior generation -- defends position at the large-plant scale.
- Gross margin structurally 64-67%: indicative of genuine pricing power and IP-protected uniqueness.
- Emerging optionality: ERII's PX technology is now being adapted for industrial water reuse, wastewater, and lithium brine extraction -- each a nascent but real additional TAM.

**Base case (2035):** Revenue grows from GBP 194M to GBP 300-350M (4-5% CAGR via organic + tuck-ins); margin expansion to 11-13% net; net income GBP 35-45M; at 22x P/E -> market cap GBP 770-990M. Stock: 2-2.5x from current GBP 392M.

**Bull case:** PFAS regulatory wave drives Laboratory division to accelerate; aerospace recovery adds volume; one meaningful acquisition doubles the Metal Melt Quality franchise. Revenue GBP 400M, net margin 14%, earnings GBP 56M, 25x P/E -> market cap GBP 1.4B. Stock ~3.5x.

**Downside floor:** Net cash, long-standing quality moats, 0.48 beta, and 40-year operating history with consistent profitability. Floor: ~16x P/E on current earnings = ~650p. Modest downside (-23%) from current price.

**Vista-pattern check:** MODERATE asymmetry. Not a 10x story but a genuine quality compounder at a fair entry with below-market volatility. Think "Halma light" -- a portfolio of narrow-moat industrial niche businesses.

### Top 3 Risks
1. Low growth: Revenue effectively flat for 2 consecutive years (2024-2025 +0.7%). If core markets don't recover, the investment case stalls.
2. AIM illiquidity: Low daily volume (~GBP 200K/day estimated) means a significant stake is difficult to build or exit. Position sizing constrained.
3. Segment concentration: ~30-35% of revenue in Metal Melt Quality ceramic filters (aluminum castings) -- a cyclical end-market tied to auto production.
