# Silicon Photonics / Optical I/O / Co-Packaged Optics Supply Chain

_Lane 21 — light-based interconnect enabling AI datacenters. Smaller/earlier names, not the obvious large-caps. Not Coherent (COHR, ~$10B cap, complex conglomerate), not Broadcom, not Nvidia. Fabrinet (FN) already covered in Lane 05._

**Informational research synthesis. Not financial advice. All figures sourced and flagged where unverified. As of 2026-06-01.**

---

## Why this lane, why now

The AI datacenter is hitting a physics wall. Copper interconnects cannot move data at 800G/1.6T speeds without burning prohibitive power. The industry answer is silicon photonics and optical I/O. Every GPU cluster being built today or planned through 2030 requires photonic transceivers and eventually co-packaged optics (CPO). The wave is supply-constrained for at least 2026-2029 (McKinsey: 800G shortfall 40-60% through 2027; TrendForce projects AI optical transceiver TAM doubles from $16.5B to $26B in a single year, 2025 to 2026). This lane hunts the picks-and-shovels one and two levels below the obvious names.

**Key supply chain taxonomy:**
1. Substrate wafers: the starting material (Soitec Photonics-SOI)
2. Compound semiconductor growth equipment: tools that grow InP/GaN (Aixtron MOCVD)
3. SiPho foundry services: fabbing the photonic chips (Tower Semiconductor)
4. Component/module IC design + InP photonics: analog front-end, EMLs, photodetectors (MACOM)
5. Precision optical assembly/packaging: Fabrinet (already in Lane 05)

## GEM 1: SOITEC (SOI -- Euronext Paris)

**France | ~EUR6.3B market cap (~$6.8B) | Trough, photonics inflecting**

### Wave and why under-covered / early

Soitec holds >95% global market share in Photonics-SOI wafers -- the starting material for every silicon photonics chip. When Nvidia, Broadcom, Marvell, or Cisco fabricates a silicon photonics transceiver chip, there is a ~95% probability that chip was made on a Soitec wafer. The AI datacenter buildout is driving pluggable 400G/800G/1.6T transceivers at unprecedented scale, with co-packaged optics (CPO) as the next step where the photonic engine sits inside the switch package. Soitec Photonics-SOI wafers are used in pluggable transceivers and are in development qualification for CPO at all major hyperscalers. The photonics segment crossed $100M revenue in FY2026 (fiscal year ending March 31) ahead of schedule.

Why under-covered: Soitec headline revenue fell 34% in FY2026 to EUR592M because its much larger RF-SOI segment (mobile phones) is in a severe smartphone inventory correction. Investors misread the mobile trough as a company-wide structural problem. Until the FY2026 results week (May 27, 2026, stock +14% on the day), the stock was down 75% from its 2021 peak, trading at trough multiples on severely depressed revenues. This is a high-quality monopoly business priced like a cyclical commodity. Bank of America March 2026 optical interconnects primer cited Soitec as holding >95% photonics-SOI share. [Source: photoncap.net citing BofA; Soitec May 27 2026 press release]

### Moat

1. Smart Cut patent portfolio + 30 years of process know-how: Soitec invented Smart Cut (hydrogen ion implantation and thermal cleave at atomic precision) and holds ~4,300 active patents worldwide. Shin-Etsu and GlobalWafers have Smart Cut licences but have no meaningful Photonics-SOI production. The process know-how required for 220nm silicon layer uniformity within +/-1nm across a 300mm wafer has taken Soitec decades to develop. [Source: photoncap.net; jasonschips.ai; convequity.com -- independent analysis consistent with company disclosures; unverified independently]
2. Switching costs: Each chip manufacturer runs 12-24-month qualification tied to Soitec wafer spec; re-qualifying a new wafer supplier means restarting all process development. Hyperscaler AI capex timelines make this prohibitive.
3. Photonics-SOI wafer recycling economics: The Smart Cut donor wafer is recycled across multiple production runs, creating a cost structure competitors without this IP cannot match.

### Founder / Insider ownership

Not founder-led (founded 1992 by CEA-Leti researchers). New CEO Laurent Remont appointed April 1, 2026 from Infineon. Key shareholders: CEA Investissement ~11%, Bpifrance Participations ~11%, National Silicon Industry Group (China) ~11%, retail 35%, institutions 35%. Management insider ownership low (<1%). CEA/Bpifrance strategic-investor presence provides governance stability and national-champion protection; China stake is a monitoring flag. [Source: Soitec IR shareholding structure page]

## GEM 2: AIXTRON (AIXA -- Xetra Frankfurt)

**Germany | ~EUR5.6B market cap (~$6.1B) | Trough, InP photonics inflecting**

### Wave and why under-covered / early

Aixtron makes MOCVD (metal-organic chemical vapour deposition) equipment -- the deposition tools that grow compound semiconductor crystal layers (InP, GaN, SiC, GaAs) atom by atom. You cannot make EML lasers, photodetectors, VCSELs, or GaN power transistors without MOCVD tools. Aixtron is the dominant global provider:
- ~90% share of the G10-AsP platform: the specific MOCVD tool that grows InP on 6-inch wafers at the volume required for AI photonic laser production [Source: Kerrisdale Capital long thesis Nov 2025; Compoundsemiconductor.net]
- ~70-90% overall MOCVD market share across all compound semiconductor product families [Source: Kerrisdalecap.com]

The wave: The entire AI optical interconnect supply chain (Lumentum, Coherent, and all InP laser makers) must expand InP production capacity 5-10x through 2030. Every expansion requires Aixtron tools. Lumentum placed multiple G10-AsP orders directly with Aixtron in May 2026 [Source: semiconductor-today.com May 2026; Aixtron press release via webdisclosure.com] -- a direct, live signal that the AI-driven InP capex cycle has started. Aixtron also has exposure to GaN-on-SiC datacenter power (Nvidia 800V architecture) from the same tools ecosystem.

Why under-covered/early: Aixtron stock collapsed ~50% from its 2021-2023 peak. The EV power electronics (SiC/GaN) cycle trough hit simultaneously with a broader semi-equipment correction. Kerrisdale Capital published a bullish long thesis in November 2025 calling it "the most underappreciated AI beneficiary in the market." Q1 2026 order intake surged 30% YoY with 65%+ from optoelectronics. Management raised FY2026 guidance (to ~EUR560M revenue, up from EUR520M). The inflection is just beginning.

### Moat

1. 90%+ share in G10-AsP InP MOCVD tools: Aixtron IS the InP production capacity expansion for the global laser industry. Customers cannot switch mid-cycle; tools run 24/7 and require Aixtron service/process engineers.
2. Process knowledge / co-development: Aixtron develops tool recipes jointly with customer process engineers. This co-development creates deep IP entanglement -- switching to Veeco means restarting materials growth from scratch with a new partner's recipes.
3. GaN/SiC power dual-moat: The same equipment leadership applies to GaN power transistors for data center PDUs (Nvidia 800V) -- a separate but equally large opportunity.
4. Long tool delivery cycles: Lead times for MOCVD systems run 12-18 months; customers must order 2+ years ahead. This creates backlog visibility and reduces cyclicality.

### Founder / Insider ownership

Founded 1983 (RWTH Aachen spin-out). No single founding family as large current holder. CEO: Dr. Felix Grawert (appointed 2019). Insider ownership very low (<1% identifiable). Institutional ownership ~64%; retail/public 36%. Not founder-led. [Source: Yahoo Finance ownership; Simply Wall St]

## GEM 3: TOWER SEMICONDUCTOR (TSEM -- Nasdaq)

**Israel/US | ~$28-30B market cap | Inflecting -- $1.3B SiPho contracted for 2027**

### Wave and why under-covered / early

Tower is the dominant independent silicon photonics foundry -- the contract fab that manufactures photonic integrated chips designed by Broadcom, Marvell, Nvidia ecosystem partners, Applied Optoelectronics, and 50+ other active customers. The key dynamic: TSMC, Samsung, and GlobalFoundries are primarily logic/memory fabs; their SiPho capabilities are nascent and captive. Tower built its SiPho platform (PH18) over 15+ years and is widely cited as the only production-grade independent SiPho foundry. [Source: Seekingalpha "Tower Semiconductor: The Hidden AI Photonics Winner"; Convergedigest.com]

The proof: On May 13, 2026, Tower announced $1.3B of contracted SiPho revenue for 2027 alone, backed by $290M in customer prepayments. A larger contractual commitment for 2028 was also signed with additional prepayments due January 2027. 50+ active SiPho customers. Total demand exceeds contracted backlog. [Source: Tower IR press release May 13, 2026; GlobeNewswire]

Why under-covered: Tower is a Nasdaq-listed Israeli company sitting in the "specialty analog foundry" box in most institutional portfolios. Its SiPho segment was a rounding error until 2025 (SiPho revenue $228M in FY2025, doubling from $106M in FY2024). The $1.3B contracted 2027 figure only arrived May 13, 2026 -- not yet embedded in most investor models. The Intel acquisition (announced 2022, terminated August 2023 after China antitrust failure, with Intel paying $353M termination fee) left the stock in institutional limbo as a "failed takeout." [Source: Tower IR; Wikipedia; Motley Fool]

### Moat

1. PH18 production-grade SiPho PDK: Tower's Process Design Kit has been qualified by 50+ customers over 15+ years. Qualifying a new foundry requires 2-3 years and is practically infeasible mid-supply-crunch. [Source: Convergedigest; Tower earnings calls]
2. 200mm + 300mm multi-fab SiPho capacity: Tower operates 200mm SiPho fabs in the US and Israel + a 300mm SiPho fab in Japan (NuTower JV with Panasonic). Geographic diversification matters for supply-chain sovereignty.
3. NVIDIA co-development: Tower and Nvidia announced joint work on 1.6T silicon photonics for AI data center optical modules (February 2026). [Source: Tower IR press release Feb 5, 2026]
4. Specialised InP + TFLN modulators: Tower supports InP-on-silicon and thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) modulators -- performance differentiators that TSMC integrated CMOS+SiPho cannot match in modulator performance.
5. Customer-funded capacity: $290M in customer prepayments substantially de-risks the $920M capacity expansion (targeting 5x SiPho capacity by end of 2026).

Management praised for operational transformation from near-bankruptcy. No controlling shareholder; arm's-length governance. [Source: Tower IR; Simply Wall St; Globe and Mail Ellwanger profile]

## GEM 4: MACOM TECHNOLOGY SOLUTIONS (MTSI -- Nasdaq)

**US | ~$29B market cap | Growth inflection: data center 60%+ YoY**

### Wave and why under-covered / early

MACOM is a US analog/photonic semiconductor designer with a US DoD Category 1A Trusted Foundry designation and unique InP photonics + GaN-on-SiC foundry capability. In the photonics supply chain, MACOM occupies a specific niche: it designs and manufactures the analog front-end ICs for optical modules -- specifically transimpedance amplifiers (TIAs) and photodetectors that receive the optical signal, plus continuous-wave (CW) lasers for silicon photonics light sources. For 1.6T LPO (linear-drive pluggable optics) modules, MACOM bundles its TIA + photodetector as a complete analog front end -- a key differentiator vs selling individual ICs.

Why under-covered: MACOM is classified as a "defense/RF/industrial" company by most sellside analysts. Its defense segment (43% of revenue) with GaN-on-SiC radar, EW, and satellite components creates the impression of a slow-growth government contractor. The data center transition from near-zero to 60%+ growth in FY2026 happened faster than sellside category updated. MACOM hit a $1B annual revenue run rate in FY2026 Q1 for the first time in its history. The inflection is recent.

### Moat

1. Trusted Foundry (DoD Category 1A) + InP vertical integration: Only a handful of US fabs hold DoD Category 1A -- MACOM has two. This creates a dual revenue stream: commercial photonics + defense electronics in the same fab, cross-subsidising fixed costs.
2. TIA + photodetector bundled analog front-end: For 1.6T LPO modules, MACOM's bundled solution creates stickier customer relationships than selling individual ICs.
3. 200G photodetector + CW laser expansion: One of very few companies with both the design capability and own InP fab to produce 200G-per-lane photodetectors needed for 1.6T. RTP fab integration adds 30% InP capacity by late 2026.
4. Book-to-bill 1.3x (Q1 FY2026): Orders exceeding deliveries; backlog building. [Source: MACOM Q1 FY2026 earnings call]

### Founder / Insider ownership

No founder alignment. CEO Steve Daly (appointed 2019) holds ~55,826 shares (~0.04%) -- low. Net insider selling in last 12 months. [Source: Gurufocus MTSI insider data] Institutional ownership dominant. The key alignment is structural (DoD Trusted Foundry status creates revenue stickiness), not management incentive alignment.
