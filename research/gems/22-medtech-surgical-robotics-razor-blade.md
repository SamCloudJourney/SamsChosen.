# Lane 22 — Medtech Devices & Surgical-Robotics Razor-and-Blade

_Researched 2026-06-01. Informational synthesis only — not financial advice. All figures from SEC filings, earnings releases, stockanalysis.com, and verified cross-sources; flagged where uncertain._

**Lane thesis:** Medical device companies with a "razor-and-blade" installed-base model (capital equipment or implant placement → obligatory recurring consumables / handpieces / disposables) compound quietly for decades. Once a hospital system integrates the capital platform, switching costs are regulatory, clinical, and logistical — the consumable stream is near-annuity. The best of these generate ROIC well above WACC for 10–20 years.

**Note on LeMaitre Vascular (LMAT):** Already a 4/5 conviction pick in Lane 14 (medtech/vet/dental). Not re-covered here.

## GEM 1: HAEMONETICS (HAE)

**NYSE: HAE | US | ~$2.8B market cap | Profitable compounder**

### Wave & why under-covered

Global plasma collection is a ~$30B+ structural growth market: plasma-derived therapies (immunoglobulin, albumin, clotting factors) are irreplaceable biologics with no synthetic substitute and ~7-9% annual volume growth driven by rare-disease diagnoses, expanding indications, and EM middle-class access to IVIG. Haemonetics owns the data-and-capital layer of plasma centre operations — invisible to most generalist investors because it is framed as "medical devices" not "biologics infrastructure." ~16 analysts cover HAE, yet it trades at 16x P/E vs the 27x healthcare peer average.

### Moat

The NexSys PCS plasma collection system and the NexLynk donor management software form a joint lock-in moat that is among the most durable in all of medtech:

1. **Regulatory switching barrier:** Switching from NexSys to any competitor requires the plasma centre operator to re-validate its entire plasma collection and fractionation process with the FDA. This is a 12-24 month project with six-figure cost — done almost never. Haemonetics traces this lock-in back 40+ years; some NexSys installs will outlive careers of people who installed them.
2. **Software integration:** NexLynk is embedded in donor scheduling, yield tracking, and compliance workflows. Even if a centre wants different hardware, pulling NexLynk is a parallel IT project.
3. **Network-level stickiness:** The dominant global fractionators (CSL, Grifols, Octapharma) have multi-year supply agreements through which they indirectly standardise collection equipment at their franchised centres — further embedding HAE.
4. **Handpiece/disposable recurring revenue:** Each NexSys placement drives a recurring set-stream of proprietary disposable kits — the classic razor-blade mechanic. Disposables are ~100% of Plasma revenue (the NexSys machines are provided at low/no cost to drive kit volume).

Secondary franchise: Interventional Technologies (vascular closure devices — VASCADE, SafeGuard). Growing 10%+ organically; further leverages hospital-procedure recurring revenue.

### Founder / insider alignment

No founder; institutional-dominant ownership (Vanguard, BlackRock). No deep insider alignment. Flag: this is a modest negative vs the ideal profile.

### 10-year bull case & asymmetric math

**Base case:** CSL headwind absorbed by 2027. Global plasma collection grows 7-8% annually. HAE captures incremental kit volume as NexSys installs compound. Interventional Tech adds 10% organic per year. By FY2031, revenue ~$2.0B, operating margin expands to 20%+. FCF ~$300M. At 18x FCF: $5.4B market cap = ~2x.

**Bull case:** Plasma biologics re-rate as rare-disease diagnoses accelerate; HAE captures EM plasma network buildout (EM IVIG demand growing 15%+/year). NexSys with Express Plus/Persona PLUS increases per-centre kit consumption 15-20% without new installs. Revenue $2.5B+, FCF $400M+. At 22x FCF: $8.8B = ~3x. Timeframe: 7-10 years to bull.

**Asymmetric floor:** NexSys installed base has a regulatory switching barrier — FCF is highly predictable. Floor ~$35-40 (trough P/E), so ~35-40% downside vs 200-300% upside.

### Top 3 risks

1. **CSL volume step-down (known, near-term):** Largest single customer transition creates 1-2 year revenue headwind. Execution risk is real.
2. **Plasma biologics disruption:** If recombinant alternatives reduce plasma demand, the growth flywheel slows. Medium-term risk; immunoglobulin demand structurally durable.
3. **No founder alignment:** Management without meaningful skin in the game is a discipline risk long-term.

## GEM 2: AMBU A/S (AMBU-B)

**Nasdaq Copenhagen: AMBU-B | Denmark | ~$2.9B market cap (~DKK 20B) | High-growth, emerging profit**

### Wave & why under-covered

Single-use endoscopy is a structural platform shift. Reusable endoscopes (Olympus, ~70%+ market share) require complex 50+ step reprocessing between patients, with documented infection transmission risk. Single-use endoscopes eliminate cross-contamination entirely. Ambu is the global #1 in single-use endoscopy, yet obscured by: Danish/Copenhagen listing, non-US domicile, and historically unprofitable status. Market growing at 16-23% CAGR (multiple independent forecasts converge). Q1 FY2025/26 Endoscopy Solutions grew +14.4% organic (Urology/ENT/GI +21%).

### Moat

1. **First-mover in a structurally-transitioning market:** Ambu has been building single-use endoscopy since 2014. It holds proprietary regulatory clearances across dozens of SKUs. Each clearance took 3-5 years; competitors face the same timeline.
2. **Procedure-level economics:** The scope IS the consumable (priced ~$500-2,000). Every endoscopic procedure creates a recurring sale with no separate capital equipment step.
3. **Clinical familiarity and friction:** Endoscopists trained on Ambu aScope gain muscle memory and procedural comfort specific to Ambu's product. Product-switching has meaningful friction.
4. **Portfolio breadth:** Bronchoscopes, duodenoscopes, gastroscopes, cystoscopes, ureteroscopes, ENT — broadest single-use endoscopy portfolio, enabling cross-selling across hospital specialties.
5. **Endorsing the market shift:** Olympus, Boston Scientific all entering single-use validates the direction — but Ambu has 7-10 year head start.

### Founder / insider alignment

No active founder. KIRKBI A/S (Lego family holding company) is a ~23% anchor shareholder [flagged: verify exact %]. KIRKBI provides patient long-term capital — 100-year-horizon industrial family ownership is a meaningful governance positive even without an operational founder.

### Sourced economics

- TTM revenue (to Mar 2026): ~$947M (~DKK 6.5B)
- FY2024/25 revenue: ~$810M (DKK ~5.6B) — ~18% growth
- Organic growth guidance FY2025/26: 10-13% overall; Endoscopy Solutions ~15%
- EBITDA: DKK 1.04B → EBITDA margin ~19%
- EBIT margin (Q1 FY2025/26 reported): 10.5%; full year guidance 12-14% before special items
- FCF: Transitioning from negative to positive; no clean FCF figure verified for most recent year. Flagged as unverified.
- Gross margin trajectory: improving with scale as single-use scope manufacturing matures

### Valuation

- Market cap: ~DKK 20B (~$2.9B USD) as of early 2026.
- EV/Revenue: ~3x (approximate estimate, unverified precisely).
- At 15%+ revenue CAGR: Revenue could reach $2.0-2.5B+ by 2030 with 20-25% EBIT margins → EBIT $400-600M → 15x EBIT gives EV $6-9B vs today's ~$2.9B cap. Approximately 2-3x in 4-5 years on base case.

### 10-year bull case & asymmetric math

**Bull case:** Single-use endoscopy reaches 40-50% of all flexible scope procedures by 2035 (from ~8-10% today). Ambu's hospital relationships compound scope volumes across specialties. Revenue $3-4B+ by 2035, 22-25% EBIT margin, FCF $500-700M. At 25x FCF: market cap $12.5-17.5B = 4-6x from today. Structural driver: infection control mandates, liability avoidance, and hospital workflow efficiency.

**Asymmetric floor:** DKK 20B market cap on DKK 6.5B revenue. Even at 7% growth, hospital relationships persist. Floor ~DKK 10-12B (1-1.5x distress revenue). Downside ~40-50% vs upside 400-600%.

### Top 3 risks

1. **Reimbursement pressure:** Single-use scopes cost more per procedure; payers may resist premium pricing.
2. **Olympus/Boston Scientific response:** Olympus entering single-use with full distribution network could erode Ambu's pricing advantage.
3. **FX headwind:** Revenue in USD/EUR but reporting in DKK. FX has already compressed reported growth to 3.2% despite 8.6% organic in Q1. Danish krone strength could mask business quality.

### UK ISA buyability

Ambu B shares listed on Nasdaq Copenhagen (Danish exchange). EU-listed individual common shares are not subject to US PRIIPs restrictions. Hargreaves Lansdown lists Ambu B (confirms UK broker availability in principle). Trading 212 specific support for Copenhagen exchange: **Flag — likely buyable but verify directly with T212 before acting.** Not an ADR, not a US ETF. Treat as "probably buyable; needs platform confirmation."

### Portfolio fit

Replaces **VinaCapital Vietnam (4%)** — higher quality, clearer moat, more durable compounding path vs frontier fund exposure. Trade-off: losing EM frontier diversification. **Conviction: 3.5/5.** Becomes 4/5 when EBIT margin crosses 15% sustainably.

## GEM 3: ARTIVION (AORT)

**NYSE: AORT | US | ~$1.4-1.7B market cap | Profitable, de-leveraging aortic surgery pure-play**

### Wave & why under-covered

Aortic disease (aneurysm, dissection, stenosis) is underdiagnosed, undertreated, and growing with an aging population. Artivion (formerly CryoLife) is the only company offering a complete aortic arch solution suite. Total addressable market ~$13.7B (company estimate). Under-covered because: (a) the 2022 rebrand confuses old CryoLife investors; (b) debt from JOTEC acquisition scared off generalists; (c) AMDS Humanitarian Device Exemption (FDA 2024) was narrowly covered.

### Moat

1. **Complete aortic portfolio:** On-X mechanical valve, JOTEC stent grafts, BioGlue surgical sealant, preservation services, AMDS hybrid prosthesis. No competitor has all of these.
2. **Tissue preservation:** 30+ years of FDA-regulated cryopreserved tissue processing creates regulatory and clinical trust.
3. **AMDS (Aortic Arch Repair System):** FDA Humanitarian Device Exemption 2024; clinical data showing 72% reduction in all-cause mortality and 54% fewer major adverse events vs open surgery.
4. **Sales force tenure:** Average cardiac surgery rep tenure >10 years at Artivion = surgeon relationships are decade-long partnerships.
5. **BioGlue sealant:** Used in >1 million procedures; dominant consumable in cardiac surgery — classic disposable repeat-purchase.

### Founder / insider alignment

CEO James Mackin owns ~766K shares (~1.8%, ~$27M). Total insider ownership ~5.7% (~$113M). Recent insider selling noted — mild negative flag. No founder-controlling stake.

### Sourced economics

- FY2025 adjusted EBITDA: $89.6M (+26% YoY)
- FY2026 guidance: Revenue $480-496M (+7-11%); adjusted EBITDA $100-107M (+12-20%)
- Net debt Dec 31, 2025: ~$150M net (1.8x net leverage, down from 3.8x a year earlier). Rapid de-leveraging.
- FY2026 FCF: Expected slightly positive (first year of meaningful FCF)

### Valuation

**Asymmetric floor:** $443M revenue, $90M EBITDA growing 12-20%/year. Floor ~$600-800M (1.5x revenue distress). Downside ~50-60% vs upside 150-300% — modest asymmetry.

## GEM 4: PROCEPT BIOROBOTICS (PRCT) — "Next Vista" sub-theme

**Nasdaq: PRCT | US | ~$1.5-1.6B market cap | Pre-profitability, EBITDA+ target Q4 2026**

### Wave & why under-covered

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) affects ~50% of men over 60 — 200M+ globally. Traditional treatments (TURP, laser TURP, UroLift, Rezum) have significant side-effect profiles. Aquablation (PROCEPT's robotic water-jet ablation) shows superior sexual function preservation vs TURP. The market is enormous, underpenetrated, and PROCEPT has the only FDA-cleared waterjet robotic platform for BPH. Under-covered: loss-making (screens out of quality filters), "just urology" vs oncology robotics glamour, crowded surgical robotics thesis.

### Moat

1. **Only waterjet robotic system for BPH:** AquaBeam is FDA-cleared and CE-marked. No comparable competitor exists; 3-5 year development + clearance timeline for any entrant.
2. **Installed-base handpiece flywheel:** 718 US systems at year-end 2025 (+42% YoY). Each system generates ~$3,500 handpiece revenue per procedure at ~1:1 handpiece-to-procedure ratio. Handpiece revenue +50% YoY (Q3 2025).
3. **Clinical lock-in:** AquaBeam's imaging/targeting workflow creates surgeon preference and training-investment stickiness.
4. **Reimbursement moat:** Dedicated CMS CPT code and facility payment assigned. Getting a new code = 5-7 year FDA/CMS process — a substantial barrier to competitor reimbursement parity.
5. **International optionality:** Non-US installed base nascent — Europe, Middle East, Asia provide a multi-year growth runway identical to early US trajectory.

### Founder / insider alignment

Not founder-led. CEO Larry Wood appointed Sept 2025 (Edwards Lifesciences veteran). Prior CEO Reza Zadno retired + sold shares. Insider ownership ~3%. **Flag: No meaningful founder alignment; CEO transition is execution risk.**
