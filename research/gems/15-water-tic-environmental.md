## GEM 1: BADGER METER (BMI)
- US | NYSE | ~$3.6B | Mid-cap, transitioning to SaaS.

**Wave & under-coverage:** US utilities must replace ~250,000 miles of aging pipe over two decades while meeting EPA mandates (lead service lines, PFAS, real-time leak detection) — non-discretionary, federally-mandated capex. AMI cellular penetration still <30% of US utilities (decade runway). Thinly covered (9–13 analysts), $917M revenue keeps it out of large-cap indices; still modeled as "hardware" though software grew 28% CAGR 2015–25 (target 11–12% of revenue by 2030 — a re-rating event).

**Moat:** Installed-base lock-in (cellular AMI network + BEACON SaaS + meters certified together; switching = re-certification + 5–10 yr capital cycles); NSF/ANSI 61 + AWWA certification barrier; Beyond-the-Meter (SmartCover sewer-overflow, UDlive — $25B TAM vs ~$5B core); municipal procurement inertia (10–20 yr horizons).

**Ownership:** 4.3% insider (Ronald Dix 0.78%); 93% institutional; no controlling family (a risk vs pure founder names); consistent stewards.

**Economics:** Revenue TTM $897M; 2020–25 CAGR 17% (LT organic target 6–8%); op margin 18.9%; EBITDA ~24%; FCF conversion >100%; 3-yr ROIC 33%; net cash $205M; D/E 0.02; EV/EBIT 20.2x; EV/EBITDA 17.0x; P/E ~28x. Q1 FY2026 revenue −9% YoY (project timing, H2-weighted — monitor).

**Valuation:** ~$124/share, 20x EV/EBIT. A Halma-equivalent at equal ROIC/growth would trade 30–35x in Europe — the "industrial" framing suppresses it. SaaS to 10%+ of sales + SaaS re-rating could expand EV/EBIT to 28–32x while earnings grow 10–15%; 10-yr IRR ~16–20% base.

**10-yr bull:** AMI cellular to 60–70% of utilities; SaaS to 15% of sales (re-rating "industrial" → "infra SaaS"); $500M M&A war chest; EPA lead-line replacement ($3–5B cycle). Revenue ~$2B (7% CAGR), EBITDA ~$550M at 27%, 22x → ~$12B EV = ~3.3x.

**Risks:** (1) Q1-2026 deceleration if H2 doesn't rebound. (2) Itron / Xylem(Sensus) competition. (3) US infrastructure-spending volatility (IIJA funding).

**Portfolio fit:** Replace or complement **Veralto** — Veralto measures quality, BMI the distribution-network intelligence (different layers, no overlap). If one water name: BMI's lower multiple + higher ROIC tilt the math. **Conviction: 4/5.**

## GEM 2: EUROFINS SCIENTIFIC (ERF.PA)
- Luxembourg/France-listed | Euronext Paris | ~€11B | Large-cap quality at a temporary discount.

**Wave & under-coverage:** World's largest environmental + food testing lab network (~900 labs, 62,000 employees, 62 countries), persistently misunderstood/under-owned: post-COVID over-earning hangover, complexity (400+ acquisitions), perceived Martin-family governance risk, high historical capex (real-estate program ends 2027). Trades ~9x EV/EBITDA vs SGS ~18x, Intertek ~16x — a 40–50% discount for equal/superior positioning, higher growth, founder-CEO skin in the game.

**Moat:** Regulatory-mandate machine (food safety FSMA/EU law, environmental PFAS/water directives, pharma GMP, clinical diagnostics — legally required recurring spend); accreditation + customer stickiness (ISO 17025/COFRAC/UKAS per method; switching = 6–18 months revalidation; retention >90%); network density (one vendor across 60+ jurisdictions — no competitor's breadth); world's largest PFAS testing lab (EPA March-2023 drinking-water standards = mandatory testing ramp, no incremental capex).

**Ownership:** Martin family (Analytical Bioventures SCA) ~32.9% economic / ~65.8% votes; Gilles Martin built €23M (1996) → €7.3B (2025); bought €204K Mar 2025; buybacks €540M in 2025; "no-brainer to buy back at 10x EBITDA vs peers at 15–20x."

**Economics (FY2025):** Revenue €7.30B (+5%); LT organic target 6.5%; adj EBITDA margin 22.5% (→24% by 2027); operating margin (GAAP) 13.2%; FCF margin 8.8%; ROIC 8.5% GAAP (depressed by capitalized lab construction; normalises ~12–14% post-2027); net debt €3.64B (~2.3x). EV/EBIT 15.2x; EV/EBITDA 9.0x; EV/FCF 22.9x; P/E ~28x / ~15x fwd; 14 analysts.

**10-yr bull:** PFAS/water testing = permanent growing mandatory spend (Eurofins #1); real-estate capex ends 2027 → FCF €600M→€900M+; buyback machine activates; biopharma/environmental testing recover; tuck-ins at 6–8x; re-rate 9x→13–15x EV/EBITDA. Revenue €12–14B by 2035, EBITDA €3.0–3.5B, 13x → €39–45B EV (~3–3.5x).

**Risks:** (1) Gilles Martin 65.8% voting concentration / no obvious succession. (2) €3.64B net debt (2.3x). (3) Margin-recovery execution (French clinical reimbursements, lab wage inflation).

**Portfolio fit:** Replace **Sofina** or **EXOR** — fills the "TIC compounder" blind spot; "Danaher of testing" at a 50% discount to peers, founder buying. **Conviction: 4/5.**

## GEM 4: KURITA WATER (6370.T)
- Japan | TSE | ~¥958B (~$6.3B) | Under-covered by Western investors.

Japan's dominant water-treatment-chemicals + ultrapure-water (UPW) provider — the "Ecolab of water chemistry in Asia." Key wave: semiconductor fabs need UPW, and Asia is building the world's capacity (TSMC Japan, Samsung, SK Hynix, JASM, new Indian fabs — each new fab = a captive 20–30 yr UPW customer). ~5 Tokyo analysts, ~zero Western. **Moat:** UPW expertise (sub-ppb contamination, co-developed with chipmakers; switching risks fab yield loss worth billions); chemicals+systems+embedded on-site technicians ("Total Water Management"); tightening Japanese discharge regs; few global competitors (Veolia, Evoqua/Xylem) with Kurita's Asian semi specialization. **Economics:** Revenue TTM ¥403B (~$2.7B, 9m +0.9%; 5-yr CAGR 9%); op margin 14.5%; FCF margin 7.4%; ROIC 10.3% (Japanese conservative accounting + people-cost services model; adjusted for excess cash ~12–13%); net debt ¥37B; EV/EBIT 17.1x; EV/EBITDA 10.7x; P/E ~24x. **Risks:** semi-capex cyclicality (double-hit on multiple + earnings), Japanese governance opacity, JPY depreciation. **Conviction: 3/5.** Fills Asia/semi-water gap (Asia reallocation when the India ETF is replaced).
