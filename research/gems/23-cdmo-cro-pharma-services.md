## PICK 1 — SIEGFRIED HOLDING (SFZN, SIX Swiss Exchange)

**Name:** Siegfried Holding AG
**Ticker/Venue:** SFZN · SIX Swiss Exchange (CHF)
**Country:** Switzerland (Zofingen; 13-site network)
**~Mkt cap:** CHF ~3.15bn (~$3.5bn / ~€3.3bn) as of June 2026
**Stage:** Mature compounder with US expansion inflection

### Wave & Why Under-Covered / Early

Siegfried is the world's top-3 integrated small-molecule CDMO (active pharmaceutical ingredients + drug products). The structural waves:

1. **Pharma outsourcing penetration** — small-molecule outsourcing penetration is rising structurally from ~30% to 50%+ as pharma sheds assets for capital efficiency. William Blair (initiated Outperform Sep 2025) projects 5.9% CAGR for Siegfried's small-molecule sales through 2029 just from outsourcing + market growth.

2. **BIOSECURE Act reshoring (signed late 2024)** — systematic US de-risking of Chinese CDMO supply chains (WuXi AppTec, WuXi Biologics) is creating immediate demand for non-Chinese, GMP-certified alternatives. Siegfried has pristine FDA/EMA records across 13 sites and is capturing reshoring inquiries now.

3. **Controlled substances / DEA-licensed APIs** — May 2026 Noramco acquisition (Wilmington, DE) is one of the world's largest controlled-substance API facilities; DEA scheduling creates a hard moat Chinese CDMOs cannot replicate.

**Why under-covered:** Dominated by Swiss/German institutional holders; William Blair's September 2025 initiation was only the 7th analyst; near-invisible to UK/US fund managers. Not listed in the US. A genuine blind spot.

### Moat

- **Regulatory filing lock-in**: Every API and drug product is submitted in the customer's NDA/MAA citing Siegfried's specific facility, equipment, and process parameters. Switching requires 12–24 months of revalidation and re-regulatory filing. Lock-in persists for the commercial life of every molecule.
- **DEA-controlled substance capability**: Requires multi-year DEA licensing; excludes Chinese/Indian competitors from this segment structurally.
- **Integrated API + DP offering**: ~80% small molecule (drug substance) + drug product finishing (sterile vials, ampoules, tablets/capsules) — single-source vendor preference from pharma procurement.
- **BIOSECURE reshoring magnet**: 13-site GMP network in CH/DE/ES/FR/MT/US/CN — the only non-Chinese CDMO with US + European + Asian GMP redundancy at scale.
- **Noramco / Purisys acquisition (closed May 1, 2026)**: Three sites added (Delaware, Georgia, Tasmania) at EV/EBITDA <10x, capturing US controlled-substance demand and filling the geographic gap.

### Founder / Insider Alignment

No founder (company founded 1861). CEO Marcel Imwinkelried (since Jan 2024). Insiders own ~CHF 164M at current prices. Largest disclosed holder: EGS Beteiligungen AG ~7.4%. Retail 47%, institutions 44%. Not founder-led — tolerated here because the structural moat, physical-asset floor, and acquisition value are independent of management alignment.

### 10-Year Bull Case & Asymmetric Math

**Base (6–7% revenue CAGR):** Revenue reaches CHF 2.2–2.4bn by 2033; EBITDA margin 25–27%; 14x EV/EBITDA → EV ~CHF 9–10bn → equity ~CHF 8bn → ~+155% from today.

**Bull (BIOSECURE + Noramco + margin leverage):** 8–9% CAGR; margins to 28%; 16x → EV CHF 13bn → equity CHF 11bn → ~3x.

**Downside floor:** CHF 3.15bn market cap vs. 13 GMP-certified sites with regulatory credentials. Physical replacement cost of the network alone is €4–6bn. Highly acquisitive PE or Big Pharma would pay 1.5–2x revenue (~CHF 2–2.5bn) at a minimum to acquire the network — provides asset-based support even in a cyclical trough.

### Top 3 Risks

1. **FCF / leverage timeline**: Net debt 1.5x EBITDA + Noramco deal likely pushes leverage to ~2.5–3x temporarily; refinancing risk if rates spike or integration disappoints.
2. **FDA warning letter at any site**: A single facility warning halts all shipments from that site and can trigger client reviews across the network — tail risk that has hit Lonza and Catalent.
3. **GLP-1 / semaglutide pipeline crowding out**: If pharma R&D spending concentrates so heavily on GLP-1 that smaller-molecule pipeline projects stall, new-project CDMO inflows slow.

### Portfolio Fit

**Replace India ETF or VinaCapital Vietnam.** Fills the "pharma manufacturing infrastructure" blind spot. Genuinely non-overlapping with Bachem (small-molecule API/DP vs. peptide CDMO; entirely different chemistry, clients, and regulatory pathways). Siegfried provides picks-and-shovels exposure to the entire pharma outsourcing wave regardless of which drugs succeed.

Best entry on macro or pharma-sector weakness.

## PICK 2 — POLYPEPTIDE GROUP (PPGN, SIX Swiss Exchange)

**Name:** PolyPeptide Group AG
**Ticker/Venue:** PPGN · SIX Swiss Exchange (CHF)
**Country:** Switzerland (multi-site: Belgium, France, Denmark, Germany, India, US)
**~Mkt cap:** CHF ~1.26bn (~$1.4bn / ~€1.3bn) as of late May 2026
**Stage:** Recovery compounder + imminent PE-buyout optionality

### Wave & Why Under-Covered / Early

PolyPeptide is the world's #2 pure-play peptide CDMO (behind Bachem). It is **not** a Bachem duplicate — it is a second-source play on the same GLP-1 / peptide structural wave, with differentiated exposures:

- **Same structural wave as Bachem** but at a different tier: Bachem dominates ultra-high-complexity peptides (GLP-1 APIs, oncology, rare disease). PolyPeptide serves 1,000+ distinct therapeutic peptides across more diverse end-markets, including simpler GLP-1 analogs, older generics, and emerging oligonucleotides.
- **GLP-1 capacity is so large** that Novo Nordisk, Eli Lilly, and dozens of pipeline-stage developers need multiple qualified CDMOs; PolyPeptide is the natural second-source supplier, not a Bachem substitute.
- **Braine-l'Alleud (Belgium) SPPS facility** — large-scale solid-phase peptide synthesis facility hitting target utilisation at year-end 2025; peak sales potential raised to €125M from one site alone.
- **Oligonucleotide adjacency** — PolyPeptide is building RNA/DNA synthesis capability; early-mover qualification creates a second regulatory-lock-in moat before oligonucleotides scale.

**Catalyst (April 14, 2026):** PolyPeptide formally acknowledged "market rumors" and confirmed a **strategic review of options** alongside Draupnir Holding. Bloomberg named EQT and KKR as interested parties; Advent also studying a bid. Stock rallied ~50% on this news from ~CHF 26 to ~CHF 39.

**Why still interesting post-rally:** If PE acquires at sector comps (14–18x 2026 EBITDA), that implies CHF 50–72/share (+28–85% vs. CHF 39). If the review closes without a deal, Braine continues ramping, 2026 revenue +20–25%, and stock consolidates at CHF 35–45 (limited downside from the Paulsen family floor).

### Moat

1. **Regulatory filing lock-in**: Same mechanism as Bachem — 1,000+ GMP peptide processes certified across 6 sites and embedded in customer regulatory filings.
2. **70-year peptide expertise**: Founded 1945; accumulated know-how in solid-phase synthesis, purification, and scale-up is a decade ahead of new entrants.
3. **Multi-site geographic diversification**: Belgian, French, Danish, German, Indian, US sites — addresses customers' supply-chain-resilience requirements that a Bachem/Swiss-only provider cannot match.
4. **Oligonucleotide foothold**: Emerging capability in RNA/DNA synthesis creates second layer of regulatory lock-in.

### Founder / Insider Alignment

**Draupnir Holding B.V. (56% of shares)** controlled by billionaire **Frederik Paulsen** (via Cryosphere Foundation, Guernsey). Long-term committed family capital, NOT a PE exit fund — held the business for decades. Individual investors hold ~20% further. The strategic review benefits all shareholders — Paulsen's interests fully aligned with minority shareholders in maximising exit value.

## PICK 3 — SIMULATIONS PLUS (SLP, Nasdaq)

**Name:** Simulations Plus, Inc.
**Ticker/Venue:** SLP · Nasdaq (USD)
**Country:** United States (Lancaster, CA; founded 1996)
**~Mkt cap:** ~$314M as of late May 2026
**Stage:** Small-cap compounder; regulatory-software monopoly

### Wave & Why Under-Covered / Early

Simulations Plus is **not** a CDMO or CRO. It is the picks-and-shovels **biosimulation software layer** embedded inside every pharmaceutical drug-development workflow:

- **GastroPlus®**: FDA de-facto-endorsed gold standard for physiologically-based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) modelling of oral drug absorption and bioavailability. Embedded in thousands of IND/NDA filings citing GastroPlus by name. The FDA cites GastroPlus methodology in its own guidance documents.
- **ADMET Predictor®**: AI-driven absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) prediction for early-stage drug candidates — used at the hit-identification stage before molecules ever reach a CDMO.

**Why this is CDMO-lane picks-and-shovels**: Every drug that reaches a CDMO must first pass through SLP's software in the design/development phase. SLP is the silent software enabler of the entire pharma-services chain.

**AI wave catalyst**: In March 2026, Simulations Plus announced a strategic partnership with **Eli Lilly** integrating MonolixSuite into Lilly's obesity drug development workflows — a top-tier pharma endorsement that signals platform-level adoption. SLP is building AI/ML layers on 30 years of proprietary human bioavailability databases — a data advantage no startup can replicate.

**Why under-covered**: $314M market cap; ~4–5 dedicated sell-side analysts; zero mainstream financial media coverage; invisible outside specialist pharma-informatics community. Classic micro-cap hidden-gem profile.

### Moat

1. **Regulatory lock-in**: GastroPlus models cited by name in FDA/EMA regulatory filings. Switching platforms requires formal FDA notification and years of model re-validation — effectively permanent lock-in for legacy molecules.
2. **30-year proprietary bioavailability database**: Accumulated from thousands of clinical trial outcomes; impossible to replicate without decades of pharmaceutical data collection.
3. **Scientific training network effect**: GastroPlus is standard curriculum in pharmacokinetics graduate programmes globally; newly qualified pharmacokineticists arrive at pharma employers already trained on GastroPlus and mandate its continued use.
4. **FDA institutional endorsement**: FDA guidance documents cite GastroPlus-based approaches as acceptable methodology; this institutional endorsement is non-transferable and cannot be replicated quickly by competitors.
5. **High switching cost + low churn**: Multi-year enterprise SaaS contracts; ~60% of revenue is software (gross margin ~85%); churn estimated <5% annually (unverified — flag for re-check).

### Founder / Insider Alignment

Founded 1996 by **Walter Woltosz** (ALS mission origin — capital from assistive technology patent settlement). Walter Woltosz remains a **Director and ~10% owner** as of latest filings. Founder influence on culture and product direction is institutionally embedded even as the company professionalises. Gradual insider sales are retirement-stage, not distress.

### 10-Year Bull Case & Asymmetric Math

**Base (12% revenue CAGR):** Revenue reaches $245M by 2033; 28% EBITDA margin; 25x EBITDA = $1.7bn → 5.4x from $314M.

**Bull — "Veeva for PK modelling"**: Global pharmaceutical AI adoption mandates PBPK modelling as regulatory standard; international expansion into China/India pharma R&D; 15% CAGR to $300M; 30x EBITDA on $84M = $2.5bn → 8x.

**Downside floor**: $314M cap; net cash; FDA-embedded regulatory tools; enterprise SaaS contracts. In sector stress, 15x FCF = ~$360M (~+15% from here — minimal downside); zero debt prevents dilutive capital raises.

### Top 3 Risks

1. **Certara (CRTX) competition**: Certara's Simcyp platform is a respected PBPK alternative, particularly at top-20 pharma. If Certara adds AI features faster or wins more FDA endorsements, SLP's moat could narrow. Currently SLP is stronger at SME/mid-pharma; Certara at large pharma.
2. **AI disruption of modelling**: Long-duration risk (5–10 years): LLM-based generative AI could theoretically allow pharma companies to build bespoke PBPK models without proprietary software, weakening the subscription moat. FDA conservatism is a natural mitigant.
3. **Acquisition / acqui-hire risk**: At $314M, SLP is a natural bolt-on target for Certara, a large CRO, or EQS. Could be accretive (acquisition premium) or dilutive (absorbed into a larger platform and product sunsetting).

### UK ISA Buyability

**SLP on Nasdaq** — US common stock; T212 can hold US common equity in a S&S ISA. PRIIPs/KID restriction applies to ADRs and US-domiciled ETFs, **not** to plain US common shares. **Confirmed buyable.**

### Portfolio Fit

**Replace India ETF** — fills the "digital picks-and-shovels for regulatory drug development" blind spot. Zero overlap with any of the 15 current holdings (nearest is Bioventix, but Bioventix is a diagnostic antibody business). The $314M market cap warrants a **2–3% satellite position** rather than a core 6–9% allocation.

**Conviction: 3.5/5** — extraordinary moat clarity (highest in this lane), founder influence, right price. Limited to 3.5 because of small size, Certara overhang, and services-revenue dilution of pure-software economics.
