## Lane Thesis

EUV lithography is the one chokepoint in the entire semiconductor manufacturing chain that cannot be worked around. ASML is the acknowledged monopoly at the machine level — but beneath ASML sits a surprisingly thin layer of specialist sub-suppliers, each with their own monopoly or near-monopoly in one irreplaceable sub-system. These companies are structurally upstream of the fabless/foundry hype cycle, generate recurring revenue (consumables, service, re-qualifications), and are largely invisible to generalist investors because they do not make chips — they make the machinery and materials that make the machinery that makes the chips.

The thesis is simple: as High-NA EUV ramps from 2026–2030 and EUV wafer starts multiply (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, Micron are all adding EUV capacity), every node in this sub-supply chain benefits with superior operating leverage.

Lasertec (6920.T), DISCO, Hoya, Mitsui Chemicals, TOK — all Japan-only — are blocked as direct equity ISA purchases. This materially shapes the shortlist.

### GEM 1 — Entegris (ENTG)

**Name:** Entegris, Inc.
**Ticker/Venue:** ENTG — NASDAQ (US common stock)
**Country:** USA
**~Market Cap:** ~$22B (June 2026)
**Stage:** Large-cap established; margin-recovery / debt-paydown inflection underway

#### Wave + Why Under-Covered/Early

Entegris is the dominant provider of the liquid-phase supply chain for chip manufacturing: ultra-high-purity filtration, specialty chemicals, deposition materials, EUV photoresist filtration, metal oxide resist (MOR) precursors, CMP slurries and pads, and precision materials handling. As EUV nodes advance from 7nm to 3nm to 2nm to sub-2nm, contamination tolerance drops exponentially — which means Entegris's content-per-wafer rises dramatically even if wafer starts stay flat.

Under-coverage angle: Entegris went through a painful digestion period after the 2022 acquisition of CMC Materials ($6.5B deal that added massive debt). Between 2022 and 2025 the stock was weighed down by leverage, divestiture noise, and semiconductor cycle headwinds. As of 2026, those headwinds are clearing: (i) net leverage is now ~3.8x and declining (paid down $300M in 2025 alone), (ii) capex cycle peaked — the company guides to "$1B+ in incremental revenue with limited further investment" from existing capacity, (iii) content-per-wafer secular growth is re-accelerating as customers move to GAA/High-NA EUV. The EUV cross-licensing partnership with JSR/Inpria (announced May 2026) signals Entegris is positioning in metal oxide resists — the next-gen EUV photoresist — not just legacy chemistries.

#### Moat

- 3,200+ active patents (confirmed, 2025 data) across materials science and purity technology
- Customer qualification stickiness: Entegris filtration membranes, process chemistries, and deposition gases are re-qualified node-by-node. Switching costs are enormous — a contamination event in an advanced logic fab costs the customer hundreds of millions in lost yield
- Content-per-wafer escalation: every new process step at advanced nodes adds a new Entegris SKU. The company estimates significant per-wafer content growth is structurally embedded in the 2nm/angstrom roadmap
- R&D running at ~10% of revenue ($329M in 2025), keeping it a node ahead of potential entrants
- Near-monopoly in EUV photoresist point-of-use filtration (physical membrane plus process knowledge)

#### Founder/Insider Ownership

Low — Entegris is a professional-management company with no founder. Bertrand Loy (13-yr CEO) retired August 2025; David Reeder (ex-CFO Chewy, ex-GlobalFoundries, ex-TI, ex-Broadcom) is the new CEO. Institutional ownership dominates. Not an alignment story; the moat and content-per-wafer mechanics are the buy case. [Unverified: specific insider % — flag for independent proxy check]

#### 10-Year Bull Case + Asymmetric Math

Semiconductor wafer start volumes grow at ~4–6% CAGR; Entegris's content-per-wafer grows at ~8–12% CAGR as nodes advance. Net: 12–18% revenue growth CAGR on a $3.2B base — $9–12B revenue by 2032–2033. At 30% EBITDA margins (achievable once debt clears and mix normalizes) — $2.7–3.6B EBITDA. At 20x EV/EBITDA — $54–72B EV — ~$45–60B equity from ~$22B today = 2–3x in 7 years base case. Bull case (High-NA EUV ramp faster, MOR wins, margins above 32%): 4x. The "asymmetric" kicker is operating leverage — capex done, incremental revenue falls through at high margins.

Downside floor: the business is genuinely essential. Even in a severe semiconductor cycle, fab utilization does not go to zero, filtration is replaced on schedule, and chemicals are consumed regardless. Floor is ~$10–12B market cap at severe trough.

1. Leverage: $3.5B net debt is real. A prolonged semiconductor downturn combined with interest rate stress could tighten the balance sheet. The 2022 CMC acquisition was dilutive in the near term.
2. Customer concentration: TSMC, Samsung, and Intel are dominant customers. A fab capex freeze by any one hits revenue meaningfully.
3. Competitive inroads in chemicals: Merck KGaA Electronics and Shin-Etsu are both substantial, well-capitalized competitors in photoresists and specialty chemicals. Entegris wins on purity/filtration depth but must keep investing to stay ahead.

### GEM 2 — Photronics (PLAB)

**Name:** Photronics, Inc.
**Ticker/Venue:** PLAB — NASDAQ (US common stock)
**Country:** USA (HQ Brookfield CT; global manufacturing: US, Taiwan, Korea, Singapore, UK, Germany, China)
**~Market Cap:** ~$3.2B (June 2026; note: 52-wk high $56 hit May 2026 — verify before acting)
**Stage:** Established, in an ex-cash value re-rating phase with an EUV optionality kicker

#### Wave + Why Under-Covered/Early

Photronics is the only large-scale independent ("merchant") photomask maker in the world with a significant US presence. Most leading-edge fabs make photomasks captively but cannot make enough, and will not qualify a captive mask for a competitor's process. That gap is Photronics's permanent business. It manufactures photomasks — the quartz-glass stencils through which EUV and DUV light is patterned onto silicon — across all major fabs globally.

Under-coverage / early angle: (1) The stock has been misunderstood as a mature flat business. In fact, the transition to EUV forces every fab to replace its entire mask set (billions of dollars per node). Photronics is one of only ~4 companies globally capable of making EUV masks. (2) The balance sheet is startlingly under-appreciated: $633–638M in net cash against a ~$3.2B market cap (sourced: stockanalysis.com Q4 2025 statistics). Ex-cash enterprise value is ~$1.27B on ~$860M revenue — roughly 1.5x EV/sales — and EV/EBITDA of only 4.6x. (3) Ongoing capacity expansion in Korea and the US (2025 CapEx guidance: $200M) will shift revenue mix toward high-end photomasks where margins are materially better.

#### Moat

- One of only ~4 EUV-capable mask makers globally (alongside Toppan/Tekscend and captive fab units at TSMC/Samsung). Qualifying a new photomask vendor at a leading-edge fab takes 12–18 months minimum.
- Geographic neutrality: Photronics serves TSMC AND Samsung AND Intel simultaneously — captive fab shops cannot do this. Multi-customer neutrality is a structural differentiator.
- Switching cost: an advanced photomask set costs $1–5M+. The cost of a bad mask in a live production line (yield loss, wasted silicon time) massively exceeds any savings from switching vendors.
- Capital intensity moat: the multi-beam mask writers needed for advanced lithography cost $50–100M each — barriers that only a handful of companies globally can finance.
- ROIC of ~16.5%, ROE of ~13% (sourced: stockanalysis.com, verified June 2026)

#### Founder/Insider Ownership

Insider ownership ~2.4% (sourced: stockanalysis.com statistics). No founder in active control. Institutional ownership ~96%. This is a moat-and-value play, not an alignment play.

#### Sourced Economics

- FY2025 revenue (trailing): ~$861–862M (sourced: stockanalysis.com, multiple research sources)
- FY2025 operating income: ~$211M (sourced: multiple sources)
- Operating margin: ~22.9% (sourced: stockanalysis.com)
- Operating cash flow: ~$282M; Free cash flow: ~$96M (sourced: stockanalysis.com — note CapEx is deliberately elevated at $200M/yr through build-out)
- Net cash position: $633–638M; total debt only $3.9M (sourced: stockanalysis.com Q4 2025 balance sheet)
- Enterprise value: ~$1.27B (sourced: stockanalysis.com) — EV/EBITDA of ~4.6x
- P/E trailing: ~11.9x; P/E forward: ~17–18x
- ROIC: 16.5% (sourced: stockanalysis.com)
- Q2 FY2026 results (latest): Revenue flat YoY at $210M; IC segment -5% (fab design-release delays), FPD +13%; company cited temporary headwinds but reaffirmed long-term demand outlook
- Market cap as of stockanalysis ~$1.91B (Feb 2026) to ~$3.2B (May 2026) — stock rallied sharply; verify current price before entering

#### 10-Year Bull Case + Asymmetric Math

Base case: photomask market grows 6–8% CAGR as nodes advance. Photronics's high-end mix rises from ~51% to 65%+, lifting ASPs and margins. Revenue grows from $860M to $1.5–1.8B by 2032. Margins normalize toward 28–30% as the CapEx cycle ends and operating leverage kicks in — $420–540M EBITDA. At 12–15x EBITDA — $5–8B EV — $4.5–7B equity (net cash, minimal debt). From a ~$3.2B market cap: 1.5–2.2x in 8 years on base case.

Asymmetric kicker: the market is currently pricing Photronics at trough multiples (4.6x EV/EBITDA) during a temporary demand slowdown. When the mask demand cycle turns (expected H2 2026–2027 as design releases resume and EUV ramp steps up), the re-rating toward 10–12x EV/EBITDA plus the cash hoard returning via buybacks could deliver 2.5–3.5x in 3 years on the re-rating alone.

Downside: at 4.6x EV/EBITDA and $638M net cash, the floor is extremely well-supported. Even in a worst-case scenario (global fab capex freeze, photomask volume falls 20%), the business still generates $150–180M EBITDA. At 4x — $600–720M EV — equity ~$1.2–1.4B (still ~0.4x current market cap). You need a near-catastrophic scenario to lose money here.

#### Top 3 Risks

1. High-NA EUV technology transition: the shift to 0.55 NA High-NA EUV requires new mask formats and potentially new tooling. Photronics must invest to stay qualified. Toppan/Tekscend (IPO'd October 2025 at ~$2B valuation) is a well-capitalized competitor ramping High-NA EUV masks with an IBM partnership.
2. China exposure: Photronics has a significant Xiamen facility and Chinese customer revenue. US export controls, geopolitical escalation, or loss of Chinese business could impair a meaningful portion of revenue. [Flag: verify exact China revenue % from latest 10-K]
3. Near-term IC demand slowdown: Q2 FY2026 showed IC revenue -5% YoY, citing design release delays and memory supply constraints. If this persists, the re-rating thesis extends further out.

### GEM 3 — Onto Innovation (ONTO)

**Name:** Onto Innovation Inc.
**Ticker/Venue:** ONTO — NYSE (US common stock)
**Country:** USA (HQ Milpitas, CA)
**~Market Cap:** ~$13B (June 2026; strong recent run — verify before acting)
**Stage:** Mid-cap growth; process control inflection as AI / advanced packaging demand accelerates

#### Wave + Why Under-Covered/Early

Onto Innovation is the emerging second pole in semiconductor process control — a field where KLA Corporation (~$90B market cap) dominates. ONTO has carved out defensible niches in three structurally growing areas: (1) Advanced packaging inspection/metrology (Dragonfly platform) — 3D stacking of HBM, CoWoS, advanced chiplet interconnects requires inspection at geometries that are essentially new market creation; (2) Advanced nodes wafer inspection (Atlas G6); (3) Lithography for advanced packaging — exposure tools for bump/RDL patterning, where ONTO is the market leader.

Why under-covered: KLA gets all analyst attention at $90B. ONTO's ~$13B cap means most large funds cannot build meaningful positions, and coverage is thinner. But ONTO is growing faster than KLA in the AI-packaging niche — the highest-growth segment in all of semiconductor equipment.

Under-the-radar timing: ONTO guided for >30% revenue growth in 2026, driven by >50% growth in advanced packaging. Q1 2026 (revenue $291.9M, up ~10% sequentially from Q4 2025) and Q2 2026 guidance ($320–330M) imply a ~$1.3B annualized run rate — a step-change from $1.0B in 2025.

#### Moat

- Dragonfly G5 franchise: ONTO's 3D advanced-packaging inspection system is the tool of record for many CoWoS/HBM stacking operations at TSMC, SK Hynix, Samsung. Switching costs are enormous — a yield event in an HBM stack costs the customer $500M+ in rework.
- Lithography for packaging: ONTO's tools are deeply embedded in advanced packaging lines globally. This is the segment nobody outside the industry knows about: front-end litho (ASML) is famous; back-end packaging litho (ONTO) is invisible.
- Software/analytics: ONTO sells process control software alongside tools — recurring revenue and an analytics flywheel that improves tool performance over time.
- Net-cash balance sheet: $654M cash, minimal debt as of Q1 2026 (sourced: SEC 10-Q).

### GEM 4 — Veeco Instruments (VECO)

**Name:** Veeco Instruments Inc.
**Ticker/Venue:** VECO — NASDAQ (US common stock)
**Country:** USA (HQ Oyster Bay, NY)
**~Market Cap:** ~$3.5B (June 2026; stock +209% in past year — check before acting)
**Stage:** Mid-cap; pending all-stock merger with Axcelis Technologies (expected H2 2026 close); EUV mask blank IBD monopoly is the hidden gem inside a diversified equipment company

#### Wave + Why Under-Covered/Early

Veeco's hidden gem is its Ion Beam Deposition (IBD) systems for EUV mask blanks. Every EUV mask blank manufactured anywhere in the world — by Hoya, AGC, Shin-Etsu, or others — requires the precision deposition of 40–50 alternating Si/Mo layers to create the EUV reflective coating. Veeco's NEXUS IBD-LDD system is the only commercially viable tool for this application. This is a genuine monopoly on a sub-process that is required for every EUV mask blank produced globally.

Why under-covered: Veeco is a conglomerate across multiple equipment markets (wet processing, MOCVD, laser annealing, ALD). The IBD EUV monopoly is a small revenue line buried inside a diversified company — invisible to most investors. The pending Axcelis merger further complicates the investment thesis.

Important note on merger: Axcelis Technologies (ACLS) and Veeco announced an all-stock merger October 2025 (Veeco shareholders receive 0.3575 ACLS shares per VECO share), expected to close H2 2026. The combined entity pro-forma FY2024 revenue was ~$1.7B, gross margin 44%, adjusted EBITDA $387M (sourced: Axcelis 425 filing). If merger integration complexity is a concern, this may be a pass until post-merger clarity.

#### Moat

- Virtual monopoly on IBD tools for EUV mask blanks: all leading mask blank suppliers use Veeco's NEXUS IBD-LDD system. No commercially viable alternative exists at scale. Confirmed by multiple analyst sources and Veeco's own customer win press releases.
- As EUV adoption expands (more fabs, more layers, High-NA adding incremental capacity), each new mask blank production line needs more Veeco IBD tools.
- Laser annealing tools: production tool-of-record at leading logic/memory customers for junction formation — another near-monopoly niche.
- Silicon photonics IBD (Spector platform): emerging opportunity for datacom/AI interconnects.

### GEM 5 (WATCHLIST ONLY) — Mitsui Chemicals (4183.T)

**Name:** Mitsui Chemicals, Inc.
**Ticker/Venue:** 4183 — Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE Prime)
**Country:** Japan
**~Market Cap:** ~$6.5–8B
**Stage:** Large diversified chemical conglomerate with a hidden EUV pellicle monopoly inside

IMPORTANT: Trading 212 does NOT offer direct TSE equity in its ISA. Mitsui Chemicals is available on T212 as a CFD only — NOT ISA-eligible. Included here for completeness and as a watchlist item for investors using Interactive Brokers, Saxo, or other platforms that do support Japanese equity in ISA-equivalent accounts.

#### The Hidden Gem Inside the Conglomerate

Mitsui Chemicals is the world's only commercial-scale manufacturer of EUV pellicles — the ultra-thin protective membranes placed over photomasks to prevent contamination during EUV exposure. Without pellicles, every particle on a photomask causes a fatal defect. ASML began EUV pellicle production but transferred commercial production to Mitsui Chemicals (retaining a licensing relationship). Mitsui is now the dominant commercial producer.

Next frontier: Mitsui partnered with IMEC (December 2023) to commercialize carbon nanotube (CNT) pellicles for High-NA EUV. CNT pellicles transmit more EUV photons than silicon-based ones, enabling the extreme light-source powers needed for High-NA production. Mitsui announced production facilities for CNT pellicles at Iwakuni-Ohtake Works (May 2024). This positions Mitsui as the monopoly producer of next-generation pellicles — not just current EUV.

Moat: ASML-licensed production plus 40+ years of pellicle know-how (since 1984) plus IMEC co-development partnership plus capital-intensive cleanroom facilities. No credible commercial challenger exists at scale.

Why it is a conglomerate discount: Mitsui Chemicals is a massive diversified chemical company with $11B+ in annual revenue. The EUV pellicle business is a tiny but highly profitable niche inside a commodity chemicals parent. The market prices Mitsui as a chemical conglomerate; the pellicle monopoly is not valued.

EUV pellicle market size: $558M in 2024, projected to reach $1.6B by 2030 (CAGR 14.4%; sourced: Grand View Research). Mitsui holds an estimated 50–70% share. [Flag: segment revenue within Mitsui not disclosed — unverified]

ISA Buyability: NOT BUYABLE in T212 ISA. T212 shows 4183.JP as CFD only. For investors on Interactive Brokers or Saxo that support TSE equity, this would be the most asymmetric single bet in the entire EUV supply chain — a monopoly pellicle business inside a cheap conglomerate discount.
